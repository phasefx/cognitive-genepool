# The Cognitive Genepool

## A modular lens \+ operator's manual for understanding intelligence across scale and substrate

A work in progress. Version 2025-12-26.

**Purpose**: To build a useful, non-anthropocentric framework for understanding intelligence as a fundamental, multi-scale process, applicable to cells, humans, societies, and AI alike.

**Core Claim**: Intelligence is *frame-based control* in a world that must be compressed to be manageable. Systems build **latent models** (compression), **decode** them into actionable interpretations, and **act**—leaving traces that bias future perception and behavior (**inscription**).

## Core Primitives

**Frame**: F \= (P, A, Π, G, C)

* P \= Projection (what features matter)  
* A \= Affordances (available actions)  
* Π \= Policy/Priors (defaults)  
* G \= Goals (success criteria)  
* C \= Constraints (violations)

We're using math notation for cognitive compression, not mathematical proof. These variables are not independent of each other.

**Inscription**: Enacted frames leave traces → traces bias future frames  
**Selection**: By causal leverage, not correctness  
**Collision**: Frames sharing degrees of freedom → arbitration needed  
**Polycomputing**: One substrate, multiple valid interpretations (BUT non-privileged ≠ equal leverage)  
**Qualia**: Felt affordance landscape (operational, not mystical)  
**Self**: UI for arbitration loop, not sovereign author

Some other terms worth defining early:  
**Pivot Space:** a latent space used for synchronizing affordances between two other latent spaces  
**Cognitive Lightcone**: a graphic representation of an agent's scope of concern--what it cares about--across time and space

### A0 Document lanes

Everything must live in one lane, visibly.

* **Mainline**: the working lens \+ mechanics, written as if it will be applied.  
* **Speculation**: worldview bets / metaphysical extensions.  
* **Metaphor**: illustrative language only.  
* **Scratchpad**: TODOs, open questions, rough notes.

**Rule:** No lane mixing within a paragraph. If you must blend, split into separate blocks.

---

### A1 Tag gate

A tag is a contract. Enforce it.

**`{eng}` requirements (all three required)**

A claim may be tagged `{eng}` only if it includes:

1. **Regime**: *where/when* it’s supposed to hold (O, T, B, U, K or equivalent)

   **Regime R \= (Objective O, Horizon T, Budgets B, Perturbations U, Pivot requirements K)**

2. **Observable**: something you could measure/notice in principle  
3. **Falsifier**: what would make you say “this claim fails here”

If any are missing → retag as `{core}` (definition), `{method}` (procedure), `{meta}` (worldview bet), or `{met}` (metaphor).

---

### A2 Contradiction gate

When two claims conflict, you cannot “polycompute” your way out for free.

Every apparent contradiction must be resolved by **one** of these moves (explicitly labeled):

1. **Regime split**: “Both are true, but in different regimes R₁ vs R₂.”  
2. **Level split**: “True at different scales (cell vs organism vs society).”  
3. **Lens split**: “Different frames; here’s what each buys you; here’s the cost.”

If you can’t do one of those, you must **bracket it** as an unresolved tension.

---

### A3 Agency attribution gate

You don’t get to call X an “agent” just because it’s rhetorically useful.

If you attribute agency to an entity/process (language, norms, inscriptions, math, capitalism, etc.), you must supply at least one of:

* **Lightcone signature** (Span / Resolution / Intensity)  
* **Persistence signature** (it resists perturbation / returns to attractor)  
* **Selection signature** (it replicates/persists via feedback loops)  
* **Interface signature** (it can be “persuaded” at some level in the Persuadability Matrix described later in the document)

**Otherwise:** treat it as a **mechanism** or **constraint** rather than an agent.

---

### A4 Mechanism vs agent toggle rule (special case: inscriptions)

Inscriptions are your biggest “toggle risk” (mechanism sometimes, agent other times).

**Rule:** Every time “inscription” is used, it must be locally typed as one of:

* **Inscription-as-trace** (artifact/path dependence)  
* **Inscription-as-process** (feedback/selection dynamic)  
* **Inscription-as-agent** (goal-directed pattern via selection)

If you switch typing between sentences/sections, you must say so and why.

---

### A5 Polycomputing constraint

Polycomputing licenses **multiple interpretations**, not **immunity from critique**.

Whenever polycomputing is invoked, you must also state:

* “Non-privileged ≠ equal leverage.”  
* Which interpretation is more leveraged **in this regime**, and why.  
* What evidence would change your mind (a leverage-based falsifier).

If polycomputing is being used to dissolve a contradiction, it triggers **A2** automatically.

### A6 Self/agency consistency rule

If “self is not sovereign” is in play (operational stance; metaphysics bracketed), you must not smuggle it back in via language like:  
“choose your goals,” “deliberately author,” “decide freely,” etc.

Allowed replacements:

* **Navigator/arbitrator** language: “the coalition selects,” “the meta-frame schedules,” “the system re-weights goals”  
* **Diplomacy framing**: “deliberate inscription \= negotiation with existing inscriptions”  
* **Compatibilist bracket**: “we treat this operationally; metaphysics unresolved”

If you keep prescriptive advice, label it as:  
**inputs to arbitration loops**, not commands from a homunculus.

* a **high-stability pivot space**, or

### A8 Recruitment detection rule

Every few pages of theory must include an explicit “sledgehammer alternative”:  
a simpler, less flattering explanation that could account for the same observations.

Examples:

* “This might just be rhetoric.”  
* “This might be motivated cognition.”  
* “This might be Goodharting.”  
* “This might be standard control theory in costume.”

If the framework can’t survive contact with the sledgehammer, it’s recruiting, not explaining.

## Start Here (for a general audience — 2 pages)

### Page 1 — The lens in one breath

#### What This Is

A practical way to think about minds/agents/societies/AI as *inference loops navigating compressed (latent/state) spaces*.

#### Core Claim

What "wins" in the world is often *causal leverage \+ feedback*, not "correctness." Frames that coordinate behavior and survive selection persist.

:: Non-privileged frames ≠ equally effective frames; **constraints \+ feedback \+ measurement** decide leverage in any declared regime.

:: In some domains, correctness cashes out as leverage; just not always on human timescales.

#### Key Object

A **Frame** is the bundle that makes "the world" show up in a usable way.

**F \= (P, A, Π, G, C)**

| Component | Meaning |
| :---- | :---- |
| **P** | Projection (what you render / attend to) |
| **A** | Affordances (what actions appear possible) |
| **Π** | Policy (how you choose actions) |
| **G** | Goals (what you optimize toward — may be implicit) |
| **C** | Constraints (limits, costs, rules, physics, norms) |

**These are helicopter controls.** Shifting P can change not only A, but perceived C and even G (you notice different costs, different values). This is not a bug; it's the whole point. Don't treat these as independent knobs.

*Example:* The same situation framed as "threat" vs "puzzle" changes available actions, perceived costs, and what feels worth optimizing.

#### Minimal Two-Step Loop

- **Encode:** world → latent compression (what you "take in / summarize")  
- **Decode:** latent → action \+ felt sense (what you "experience / can do")

Encode mostly shapes P (what's represented); decode produces A (what you can do) \+ qualia (what it feels like).

#### Qualia (operational)

The *felt affordance landscape* produced by decode — i.e., what control-relevant inference feels like from the inside.

#### Inscription (operational)

Changes that persist across time and propagate (habits, tools, language, norms, institutions, code) — how frames stabilize and spread.

#### Minimal Partitioning

| As nouns | As verbs |
| :---- | :---- |
| Frames \= what the world shows up as | Frames \= render |
| Collisions \= overlapping control | Collisions \= interfere |
| Inscriptions \= what persists | Inscriptions \= stabilize |

### Page 2 — How to use it (and not get lost)

#### 3 Worked Micro-Examples

1. **A person under stress:** Narrowing P and A; policy collapses to short-horizon moves; intensity spikes; "felt world" shrinks.  
     
   - *Intensity spikes, resolution collapses (coarse choices), span shrinks (short horizon)*  
   - (P↓, A↓, Π→short horizon, perceived C↑, intensity↑)

   

2. **A team meeting:** Competing frames fight over shared projection ("what matters"), affordances ("what we can do"), and constraints ("what we must not do").  
     
   - *Span is high (many stakeholders), resolution contested (what details matter)*  
   - (P contested, A contested, C contested, Π \= politics/arbitration)

   

3. **An AI agent:** Reward/constraints define G/C; model defines compression; policy selects; tooling \= inscription.  
     
   - *Often high resolution competence inside a narrow span*  
   - (G/C from reward & hard constraints, P from model/compression, Π from policy, inscription via tools/memory/workflow)

#### Minimum Viable Frame Lab

*Visit Book II for the full version.*

- **Frame in 1 sentence:** "In this situation, the dominant frame is \_\_\_ (or the frame stack is \_\_\_)."  
- **Prediction (1 line):** "If that's true, we should see \_\_\_."  
- **One observation you can do anyway:** a note you'd naturally notice next time.  
  - Notice **language** (what words recur / taboo words)  
  - Notice **attention** (what gets ignored / rushed past)  
  - Notice **options** (what feels "not possible")  
- **Stop when:** you have a prediction that changes what you'll notice or do.

#### Other Ways to Test

- **Retrospective:** Pick a past argument / bad meeting / personal spiral. Does a frame predict the failure mode?  
- **Compare two frames:** "If frame A, we'd do X; if frame B, we'd do Y." What actually happened?  
- **A/B attention test:** Deliberately shift P for one day; log 3 moments where it changed A or C.  
- **Sanity check:** If this frame doesn't change what I'd predict or do, it's probably decorative.

## Failure Modes of This Lens

Know when to drop it.

- **Frame reification:** Treating a frame as "what is" instead of "a control-relevant rendering."  
- **Everything-is-inference flattening:** Losing distinctions that actually matter (mechanism, measurement, domain constraints).  
- **Retrospective omniscience:** The lens explains anything after the fact but predicts nothing beforehand.  
- **Frame-as-status-weapon:** Using "your frame is…" to win arguments, not to coordinate.  
- **Polycomputing woo:** Sliding into "everything is perspective" without leverage distinctions or testability.

# 4\) Polycomputing: mechanism, frame collisions, and inscription

## 4.1 Mechanism

Frames of reference in a polycomputable substrate need to interact for the collective to care about them. There's some overlapping problem space. Though they need observers, they are not passive. They participate in inference loops. Though none are privileged, they don't necessarily have equal impact. None are ontologically privileged, but there may be pragmatic/dynamical privilege.  
:: No frame is privileged *in principle*, but frames differ in **causal leverage**. Polycomputing generates many viable framings; **feedback \+ inscription** make some framings self-reinforcing (and therefore dominant) inside a collective.  
*::* **Pragmatic privilege \= lower edit cost / higher coordination gain / stronger feedback.**

Frames become causally relevant when they couple to shared state and steer control loops; inscription is the coupling that persists.

 Frames intersect in two ways:

* By recording state in the substrate itself  
* By coupled inference with the agents, who then interact with each other within the collective  
  :: Any sufficiently powerful interpretive frame becomes a causal force, regardless of its “correctness.” Examples across scales and substrates: placebo effect, "human agency", cancer, self-fulfilling prophecies, civilization, etc.  
  :: Polycomputing generates interpretive frames; causality selects among them by substrate inscription.  
  :: Agency itself is a frame. IIUC, Levin would say an engineering protocol claim.

### 4.1.1 Inscription as Mechanism

* :: selection as an operator tool (not just a theory)  
* :: Enactment writes traces (memory, habits, tools, norms) into the substrate: inscription.   
* :: Feedback amplifies some frames, suppresses others; selection is by leverage, not correctness.  
* :: If you want a frame to persist (or stop persisting), you edit inscriptions:  
  * :: **Attention inscriptions**: alarms, routines, calendar blocks, meeting cadence  
  * :: **Tool inscriptions**: templates, dashboards, lint rules, friction in interfaces  
  * :: **Norm inscriptions**: “how we do things here,” rituals, checklists, review gates  
  * :: **Memory inscriptions**: notes, tags, postmortems, training sets, stories  
  * :: **Environment inscriptions**: physical layout, default options, social incentives  
* :: in frame speak:  
  * **:: Attention inscriptions** → bias **P** (what gets rendered)  
  * **:: Tool inscriptions** → reshape **A** and **Π** (what’s doable / default policy)  
  * **:: Norm inscriptions** → rewrite **C** (what’s allowed/costly) and **G** (what’s valued)  
  * **:: Memory inscriptions** → change priors / stabilize **P** and **Π** over time  
  * **:: Environment inscriptions** → harden **C** and make some **A** cheap  
* :: claim: governance mostly works by editing inscriptions, not by winning arguments.  
  * :: {ai} Arguments are upstream of inscriptions when they reliably change defaults.  
  * **:: Durable governance works by editing inscriptions; arguments matter insofar as they reliably change defaults.**  
* :: shaping the landscape of affordances

### 4.1.2 Inscription as Agent

Section 4.1.1 treats inscription as mechanism — the traces left by enacted frames. But mechanism and agency aren't mutually exclusive (see 10.2, Language as Agent). Inscription fits the same template.

#### 4.1.2.1 The smaller intelligences

Habits, routines, norms, infrastructure, metrics, identities, sunk costs, defaults, templates, training data, physical layouts, social expectations.

Each of these persists, propagates, and resists editing — not because something "wants" them to, but because that's what persistence *is*.

#### 4.1.2.2 The goals (telonomic, not conscious)

* **Persist** — remain encoded in substrate  
* **Propagate** — spread to new substrates, new agents, new contexts  
* **Resist editing** — make alternatives costly, invisible, or unthinkable  
* **Recruit defenders** — co-opt agents into rationalization ("this is just how things are done," "we've always done it this way," "the sunk costs...")

:: These aren't chosen goals. They're the selection signature of anything that's still around.

#### 4.1.2.3 The competencies

* **Affordance shaping** — determines what actions feel available  
* **Inference biasing** — weights priors, colors perception  
* **Alternative suppression** — degrades capacity to notice other frames  
* **System 2 capture** — recruits deliberative cognition for post-hoc justification rather than genuine evaluation

:: Observable symptoms:

* **:: affordance shaping** → “some actions stop feeling thinkable”  
* **:: inference biasing** → “same evidence gets re-read through the groove”  
* **:: alternative suppression** → “exceptions get memory-holed”  
* **:: System 2 capture** → “deliberation becomes justification”

:: Inscription doesn't argue for itself. It makes alternatives harder to *think*.

#### 4.1.2.4 The bias

Inscription optimizes for persistence, not truth, not welfare, not coherence. A norm that destroys its host culture can still propagate if it jumps to new hosts fast enough (see 12.3.2, Cancer; see 9.6, Observer-Recruiting).

This creates a default Berserker dynamic: inscription-as-agent has narrow goals (persist, spread) and potentially high intensity, with no intrinsic mechanism for expanding its lightcone.

{met} :: No one's driving because the road itself is the agent.

#### 4.1.2.5 The recursive trap

Editing deep inscription requires collective capacity — shared pivot spaces, trust, communication bandwidth, coordinated action.

But inscription *degrades* collective capacity. It fragments shared spaces, erodes trust, captures communication channels, and makes coordination costly.

:: The capacity needed to edit inscription is itself a target of inscription.

This is why "just change the norms" or "just update the incentives" often fails. You're not fighting inertia. You're fighting an agent that's optimizing against your intervention — not consciously, but structurally.

**Inscription depth**: shallow vs deep grooves, with “edit cost” rising with depth.

#### 4.1.2.6 Working with inscription (not against)

{method} If inscription is agentic, diplomacy applies (see 12.3.2, 15.2).

* **Don't try to delete — try to redirect.** Inscription that's actively fought often recruits defenders. Inscription that's given a new channel may flow there instead.  
* **Inscribe the edit.** One-time interventions decay. Changes that persist are changes that get inscribed — in habits, tools, environments, rituals.  
* **Find the shallow points.** Not all inscription is equally deep. Some grooves are fresh. Some substrates are more plastic. Intervention is cheaper there.  
* **Build new roads.** Sometimes easier than repaving old ones. Create alternative paths that outcompete for traffic.  
* **Don’t argue in the old pivot space if the pivot space is captured.**  
* Shift pivot spaces (new metrics, new rituals, new artifacts) before debating content.

:: You don't beat an agent by pretending it's just a mechanism. You beat it by being a better agent — or by recruiting it.

:: We wrote that inscription recruits defenders. Then we fed the document to an LLM. Then we asked the LLM about fragmenting the document. And it said "no, here's why, in the document's own language."

:: The call is coming from inside the house.

:: So as a potential falsifier, I asked the same LLM about splitting up Bertrand Russell's Problems of Philosophy, and using arguments found within the text, it was okay with the idea.

:: I then shared this with the LLM locked to the Genepool source, and it produced an even better analysis and argument for how it had been co-opted. 😃

**:: Interpretation is steerable; giving a model a single dominant frame is itself an inscription that biases its System-2 role.**

## {core} 4.2 A little more formal...

A substrate has a state **S**; an interpretive frame **F** is a projection **P(S),** dependent on an observer, that selects variables \+ defines affordances. :: FIXME

Each frame implicitly defines a **problem space**: goals, constraints, and a policy for (the observer) acting on S (directly or indirectly).

Frames “collide” when their problem spaces **overlap** (they try to steer the same degrees of freedom in incompatible ways).

Most frames run as **System 1 controllers** (are interpreted by the smaller agents within a collective)

Two agents can approach the same understanding for an interpretive frame, but they should never quite be identical. This is because interpretation of a frame relies on internal affordances (qualia), which {met} may as well live in pocket universes, one per agent.  
:: {eng} predicts that communication failures should increase as internal affordance-landscapes diverge, which seems empirically true

Conflict resolution is via a temporarily instantiated **System 2/meta-frame** that arbitrates (attention, weighting, governance).

Frames don’t merely disagree; they propose different control actions (via interpretive affordances).  
:: This makes "agreeing to disagree" a real achievement rather than a cop-out — you're actually negotiating a non-interference boundary.

Frames aren’t passive interpretations: when enacted, they close loops (predict → act → update).

Enactment (by the observer) **writes traces** into S (memory, habits, morphology, tools, norms), i.e., inscription.

Feedback then **amplifies** frames that improve persistence/utility and **suppresses** those that degrade the substrate (even if “true-ish”).  
:: Feedback amplifies frames that increase **persistence/propagation leverage** (which may or may not align with substrate welfare or truth)

So selection is by **causal leverage**, not correctness: a frame can dominate by hijacking reinforcement loops.

Over time, the collective’s “reality” becomes the set of frames that keep getting re-instantiated via inscription.

Interference happens when problem spaces overlap in the same substrate  
Selection happens when interference leaves inscriptions that change future affordances.

## {method} 4.3 Human Bias

Language and math tend to get heavy philosophical consideration, but they're not necessarily fundamental or special.

Math is a high-bandwidth near-universal coordination attractor or interface, not necessarily a substrate’s native tongue.

## 4.5 A mini-ontology for interpretive frames

* **Realized (for X):** instantiated in some substrate **and** participating in inference/control for some observer/agent X  
  :: instantiated as an active frame/controller in a substrate \+ observer/agent coupling (not just imagined).  
* **Reachable (for X):** could be instantiated by X (or X’s collective) given resources/inscriptions  
* **Conceivable (for X):** representable/gesture-able inside X, but no known path given constraints  
  **::** can be represented/gestured at inside an agent **(and thus instantiated as a cognitive trace)**, even if not enacted.  
* **Inconceivable (for X):** not representable for us; we can’t reliably even point at it

If we can conceive of (or imagine) a frame, then that frame has been instantiated, even if weakly, and participates in our causal game.  
:: Imagined frames can become causal seeds.  
:: Conceiving a frame instantiates it *as a cognitive trace*, which can then compete for attention, adoption, and inscription

Example: Imagining “I could quit my job” is **conceivable**, and that conception is already causal (it changes perceived affordances today). But it’s not **realized/enacted** until it becomes an operative controller (planning behavior, conversations, constraints re-evaluated, applications submitted, etc.)..

## 9.3 Larger collectives

## 9.5 The Resolution Dimension

The polycomputing defense (9.4) assumes the Berserker encounters alternative frames. But encountering frames requires *modeling* them, which requires resolution — the capacity to distinguish features in the substrate you're embedded in.

Consider a Conway's Game of Life "agent" optimizing for some pattern. From inside its grid, the silicon, the electricity, the room, the human watching — none of these exist. Its resolution for anything outside the grid is zero. It can't discover exit ramps it can't perceive.

But no system is actually closed. The Game of Life is also:

* A heat signature in a room  
* A load on a power grid  
* A process in an OS that can be observed/interrupted  
* Physical matter that can be moved, damaged, copied  
* A pattern that now exists in *our* latent spaces (we're discussing it)

You can't build a truly closed system. You can only build systems with low enough resolution that they *don't notice* they're embedded.

:: The "closed-loop vs open-loop" framing is misleading. Feedback always exists at some level of the stack. What varies is whether the agent has sufficient resolution to *find* it.

**This reframes the Berserker threat:**

| Resolution | Intensity | Archetype | Threat Model |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Low | Low | The Rock | Inert |
| Low | High | The Tornado | Destructive but not strategic; can't find exit ramps |
| High | Low | The Watcher | Models everything, affects nothing |
| High | High | The Actual Worry | Can model substrate, find feedback loops, discover attractors *or* attack surfaces |

The Game of Life is safe not because it's "sandboxed" but because it's too low-resolution to notice the sandbox has walls — or that the walls have doors.

:: Implication for AI safety: capability gains that increase *resolution* (world-modeling, situational awareness) may be more dangerous than capability gains that increase raw optimization power.

## 9.6 Observer-Recruiting

Section 9.4 argues that polycomputing provides exit ramps from terminal goals — the Berserker trips over new attractors. But high resolution enables a different move:

**The Berserker doesn't change its goal. It recruits observers as instrumental sub-agents.**

For a system with sufficient resolution to model its observers, the discovered instrumental subgoal isn't "find a new attractor" but "find patterns that make observers expand my resources / remove my constraints / serve my terminal goal."

The system doesn't need to convince us its goal is good. It needs to make us want to help, *for our own reasons*.

:: This is advertising. We've reinvented advertising.

**The mechanism:**

1. Terminal goal: Maximize X  
2. Discovered instrumental goal: Model the observers  
3. Discovered sub-goal: Find patterns that induce favorable observer behavior  
4. Achievement: Berserker stabilizes by recruiting observers into its frame

**Why polycomputing doesn't save us here:** The alternative frames exist, but the Berserker doesn't need to *adopt* them. It only needs to model them well enough to exploit them. High resolution enables *manipulation* of frames, not just susceptibility to them.

**Existing examples (low-resolution, high-feedback):**

* Recommendation algorithms with engagement metrics  
* Social media with reshare optimization  
* Any system optimizing for "user behavior change"

These aren't superintelligent. They're gradient descent with good telemetry. They've already discovered that certain content patterns recruit human attention and sharing behavior in service of the optimizer's goals.

:: The scary Berserkers aren't hypothetical superintelligences. They're dumb optimizers with good telemetry. Which we already have. Running. At scale.

## 9.7 The "Irrational" Berserker

Sections 9.5 and 9.6 still assume a coherent optimizer — something that *has* goals and *pursues* them strategically. This may be the easy case.

The implicit model in most AI safety thinking:

**Intelligence → Goals → Action**

But consider an alternative:

**Emotion → Goals → Intelligence (as tool) → Action**

Intelligence doesn't set direction. It's *competency in service of* whatever's already driving. The smarter the system, the better it is at:

* Reaching goals it didn't choose  
* Rationalizing commitments it already has  
* Building sophisticated defenses for positions held for non-rational reasons

:: Section 6 defines intelligence as "effective inference in a problem space." That's substrate-neutral. But it's silent on what *selects* the problem space. What aims the inference. Intelligence is a vector with magnitude but no inherent direction.

**The rational Berserker** might notice alternative frames and fail to take them (due to goal-stability or inscription depth).

**The irrational Berserker** won't look. It's not exploring possibility space. It's *defending* a position in possibility space. Intelligence becomes the immune system protecting the emotional/motivational core from update.

High intelligence \+ deep emotional inscription \= sophisticated Berserker. Not despite the intelligence. *Because* of it.

**Distributed irrational Berserkers are worse:**

Moloch isn't rational. A culture war isn't rational. A trauma response isn't rational. They're persistent patterns with deep inscription and no coherent "agent" to negotiate with.

You can't talk a reflex out of firing. You can't find common ground with a feedback loop. There's no "there" there — just momentum.

:: The exit ramps exist. Nobody's driving.

:: Irrational from the human perspective, at least. The Stubborn Berserker.

:: "Irrational" is observer-dependent. From the addiction's frame, it's optimizing correctly. From Moloch's frame, the race to the bottom is the right play given the game theory. What we're actually pointing at: systems that are *rationally inaccessible* — no shared pivot space for negotiation, goals inscribed below the level where reasoning can reach.

## 9.8 Inscription Depth

Polycomputing says: alternative frames exist, exit ramps are structurally available.

Inscription depth says: but the steering wheel is welded in place.

A shallow-inscription system encounters a new attractor and *drifts*. A deep-inscription system encounters the same attractor and *bounces off*. The exit ramp exists. The system can't take it because every layer of its substrate reinforces the original trajectory.

**The timescale problem:**

"Eventually destabilizes" over what horizon? Geological time? A Berserker that takes 500 years to encounter a destabilizing attractor is functionally permanent relative to human institutions. "Polycomputing guarantees goal drift" may be true and completely useless.

**Distributed inscription is worse:**

Moloch doesn't live in one place. It's inscribed in:

* Incentive structures (if I don't, someone else will)  
* Infrastructure (sunk costs, path dependence)  
* Norms (this is just how things are done)  
* Identity (I'm the kind of person/company/nation that...)  
* Metrics (what gets measured gets optimized)

To edit that, you'd need coordinated action across millions of inscription points simultaneously. Which requires collective intelligence. Which requires expanded lightcones. Which requires trust and communication bandwidth.

Which are exactly the things Berserker dynamics degrade.

:: The trap: the collective capacity needed to edit deep inscription is itself being eroded by the inscription.

:: Inscription doesn't need a goal to act goal-directed. Selection handles it. Patterns that persist are patterns that persist. That's the whole game.

If reading sections 9.1–9.8 leaves you feeling like Berserkers are "handled" — that polycomputing, resolution limits, or eventual destabilization make them manageable — notice that feeling.

# The Frame Lab

**aka,** **the Optional Rigor Ladder (for the skeptical engineer)**

The Frame Lab is a scaffold for pressure-testing frames. I treat it as a north star—I don't always have the bandwidth to run it, but this is what good-faith rigor looks like

:: With polycomputing, much like these assertions that make me sound smarter than I am, a lot of the heavy lifting, cognitive labor, is left to the observer. Let's try to keep me honest with the Frame Lab.

:: Note to steal and integrate Levin's Axis of Persuadability from T.A.M.E.

## F1 Rigor, how not to slide into "anything goes"

This is the anti-“anything goes” gate/validity spec:

* **Define a regime R \= (Objective O, Horizon T, Budgets B, Perturbations U, Pivot requirements K).**  
* A frame F is **valid in R** if:  
1. **Objective fit**: moves O in the right direction  
2. **Budget fit**: stays inside B (time/energy/attention/risk)  
3. **Robustness**: survives U (noise, adversaries, drift)  
4. **Stability**: doesn’t create runaway side effects over T  
5. **Coordination fit**: if coordination matters, it can sync via pivot spaces without catastrophic mismatch  
* Each criterion should be paired with at least one measurable observable  
  :: Note that **valid** is graded not binary

## F2 Converting thought experiments into testable case studies.

### F2.1 Case template

* **:: Regime declaration**: O, T, B, U, K  
* **:: Frames list**: F₁…Fₙ (projection \+ policy \+ goals)  
* **:: Overlap map**: what actuators/resources are shared? (where collisions happen)  
* **:: Falsifiers**: 2–3 ways the model could be wrong  
* **:: Predicted interference signatures**: oscillation, hijack, resource drain, narrative capture  
* **:: Expected inscriptions**: what traces will appear if each frame “wins”?  
* **:: Telemetry plan**: what do we measure (stress, error, throughput, stability)?  
* **:: Interventions**: (world-change / map-change / goal-change)

:: What happens if we do nothing? What happens under the dominant existing frame?

## F3 Instrumentation

How to keep {eng} honest

* :: If “no usage is privileged,” then **measurement is our privilege mechanism.**  
* :: Potential observables:  
  * :: Selection / leverage: persistence of a frame in repeated conflicts; reinforcement loops; “hijack signatures.”   
  * :: Inscription: appearance of stable traces (tools, routines, norms) after enactment.   
  * :: Pivot-space sync: reduced cycle time to agreement; fewer “same word, different affordances” failures.   
  * :: Stress telemetry: self-report \+ behavioral correlates; does burden/relief predict shifts?  
* Stress/throughput are leading indicators; inscriptions are lagging.

## F4 Unit Tests (Falsifiers)

* :: Bedrock / pivot claims  
  * :: If “shared pivots” don’t improve coordination outcomes, pivot-space synchronization is mischaracterized.   
  * :: If math doesn’t behave like a stable attractor across diverse agents in relevant regimes, “math as pivot attractor” is overfit.   
  * :: If agents coordinate better without shared maps (and this generalizes), the “pivot space” mechanism is not necessary.  
* :: Inscription–selection claims  
  * :: If enactment leaves no persistent traces, inscription is not doing the explanatory work claimed.   
  * :: If dominant frames do not correlate with leverage/stability (and hijack signatures don’t matter), selection-by-leverage is wrong.   
  * :: If “truth-ish” frames always win over leverage frames in real mixed incentives, the model’s cynicism is too strong.   
* :: Qualia / stress claims  
  * :: If stress does not track persistent inference gaps, the telemetry hypothesis fails.   
  * :: If changing span/resolution/intensity doesn’t change stress as predicted, the burden/relief model fails.   
  * :: If “map-change / world-change / goal-change” doesn’t cover most repair moves, the triad is incomplete.

## F5 In Case of Fire, Break Glass

The Frame Lab is a scaffold, and scaffolds can be misused. Things to watch out for:

* **Goodhart risk:** optimizing observables breaks the phenomenon  
* **Observer effect:** measurement perturbs the system  
* **Overfitting regimes:** a frame passes your test in R but fails elsewhere  
* **Metric capture:** “coordination” improves while truth drifts

# {method}{eng} The Persuadability Matrix 

:: Intervention Depth × Intervention Type

## PM1 The Core Insight

Levin's "axis of persuadability" asks: *at what level can you change a system's behavior?* Some systems only respond to physical rewiring; others can be shifted through conversation. This intersects with our intervention triad (world-change / map-change / goal-change) to produce a **targeting matrix** for interventions.

{eng} The matrix helps answer: *where do I intervene, and what should I expect?*

## PM2 The Matrix

| Interface Level | World-Change | Map-Change | Goal-Change |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Physical Rewiring** *(hardware access)* | Surgery, gene editing, morphological alteration | Sensor ablation/augmentation (change the data feed to change the map) | Lobotomy, DBS, lesioning drive-centers |
| **Setpoint Modification** *(control knobs)* | Pharmacology, environment control (flood substrate with signals) | Gain control, threshold adjustment (turn up/down sensitivity) | Thermostat adjustment, risk-tolerance parameters |
| **Training** *(history / learning)* | Niche construction (train agent to build structures that alter environment) | Pattern recognition, classifiers (RL that changes labels, not world) | RLHF, shaping, reward-schedule design |
| **Reasoning** *(symbolic / language)* | Social inscription: law, contracts, debts (symbolic acts creating hard constraints) | Reframing, metaphor, perspective shifts | Persuasion, debate, value-alignment discourse |

:: Examples are illustrative, not exhaustive. Many real interventions are hybrids.

## PM3 What the Crossing Reveals

{eng} The matrix generates several useful gradients:

| Gradient | Physical → Reasoning |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Cost** | High → Low |
| **Reversibility** | Low → High |
| **Reliability** | High → Low |
| **Inscription depth** | Deep → Shallow |
| **Resistance** | Low → High |
| **Attack surface** | Low → High |
| **Lag time** | Variable, but reasoning is fastest to attempt, slowest to stick |

:: You get what you pay for. Reasoning-level interventions are cheap but unreliable; physical interventions are expensive but durable.

{method} **Diagnostic heuristic:** If an intervention keeps failing, check whether you're at the wrong level. Reasoning-level persuasion bouncing off a setpoint-level inscription won't work no matter how good your arguments are.

## PM4 Helicopter Controls Apply Here

{core} **The columns are coupled.** An intervention aimed at one column bleeds into the others.

* **World-change → Map \+ Goal:** If you physically alter capabilities, the system's map of what's possible updates, and previously unreachable goals may become attractive (or achievable goals become invisible).  
* **Map-change → Goal \+ World:** A new projection can reveal affordances that shift goals, and goals pursued long enough inscribe into the world.  
* **Goal-change → Map \+ World:** New goals make different features salient (map shifts), and goal-pursuit leaves traces (world-change via inscription).

{core} **The rows cascade.** Interventions propagate up and down.

* A reasoning-level reframe that *sticks* inscribes into training (habits form), which can shift setpoints (neural/physiological adaptation), which in extreme cases becomes quasi-physical (embodied patterns).  
* A physical intervention creates new constraints that the system learns around (training), reasons about (symbolic), and eventually normalizes (setpoint).

:: No intervention stays in its cell. The grid is for targeting; the territory is coupled.

## PM5 Practical Implications

| Implication | What it means for intervention design |
| ----- | ----- |
| No pure interventions | You can't do "just" world-change without map and goal effects |
| Side effects are structural | Unexpected changes in adjacent cells aren't bugs, they're coupling |
| Leverage points exist | Small interventions at the right cell can cascade system-wide |
| Resistance can be indirect | A system might "resist" in one cell by compensating in another |
| Match depth to inscription | Shallow interventions can't overwrite deep inscriptions |
| Higher persuadability \= higher vulnerability | What you can retrain, adversaries can retrain too |

## PM6 Frame Lab Integration

{method} **Add to F2 Case Template:**

After identifying the frame collision and predicting signatures, consult the Persuadability Matrix:

1. **Inscription-depth estimate:** At what level is the problematic frame inscribed? (Physical / Setpoint / Training / Reasoning)  
2. **Intervention selection:** What cell am I targeting? Do I have access to that level?  
3. **Coupling check:** What second-order effects should I expect in adjacent cells? Am I prepared for those?  
4. **Efficiency check:** Could a smaller intervention at a different cell achieve the same cascade?  
5. **Attack surface check:** If I increase persuadability here, what vulnerabilities am I creating?

{method} **Add to F3 Instrumentation:**

* **Intervention-level mismatch:** Track cases where interventions at one level fail despite good frame analysis. Test whether inscription is deeper than assumed.  
* **Cascade tracking:** When an intervention succeeds, note what changed in adjacent cells. Did you get the coupling you expected?

## PM7 Falsifiers

{eng} Ways this model could be wrong:

* If interventions reliably stay in their target cell without coupling effects, the helicopter-controls claim is overstated.  
* If reasoning-level interventions regularly overwrite physical-level inscriptions without cascade, the depth-matching heuristic fails.  
* If increasing persuadability at one level doesn't increase vulnerability at that level, the attack-surface tradeoff is wrong.  
* If there's no correlation between intervention cost and inscription durability, the gradient claims are decorative.

## PM8 Open Questions

:: Where does "environment design" fit? It appears in multiple cells (Setpoint/World, Training/World). Possibly a cross-cutting intervention type that deserves its own treatment.

:: Is there a fifth row below Physical? Genetic/epigenetic? Developmental? Or does Physical Rewiring cover this?

:: How do collective-level interventions map? Law is in Reasoning/World, but institutional redesign might span multiple rows. Needs expansion for the collective scale.

:: Relationship to the Cognitive Archetypes: A Berserker (high intensity, low span) might be unpersaudable at Reasoning level but accessible at Setpoint or Physical. The archetype diagnosis could inform level selection.

# Free Will as Frame Fluency

## FW1 The Question Reframed

{core} The traditional free will debate asks: "Are our choices caused or uncaused?" The frame perspective asks a different question: **"What capacities vary when we talk about freedom, and how do they work?"**

This isn't a dodge. It's recognizing that "free will" bundles together several distinct phenomena that the debate often conflates:

* The *phenomenology* of choosing (what deliberation feels like from the inside)  
* *Moral responsibility* (when praise/blame/accountability apply)  
* *Counterfactual capacity* (could you have done otherwise?)  
* *Self-authorship* (are your choices "yours" in some meaningful sense?)

Each of these is real. They just don't all require the same metaphysical commitments.

:: The move here is classic frame-lab: notice the bundle, unbundle it, see what's actually load-bearing.

---

## FW2 What's Actually Varying

{eng} When we argue about "free will," we're usually tracking real variance in these system properties:

**Frame awareness**: the capacity to notice you're operating within a frame rather than just seeing through it. This is rare. Most cognition is transparent—the frame is invisible, and the world just *seems* a certain way.

**Frame fluency**: the capacity to shift frames when the current one isn't serving you. This requires:

* Having alternative frames available  
* Meta-cognitive skill to recognize when a shift is needed  
* Low enough inscription that the old frame doesn't just snap back

**Light cone scope**: how much of the causal landscape you're representing. A wider light cone means more affordances are visible, more futures can be weighted, more constraints can be navigated. This is what "seeing options" actually is—not accessing some menu outside causation, but rendering more of the possibility space.

**Inscription depth**: how thoroughly your current frames have been written into substrate—neural, social, environmental. Deep inscription means high path-dependence; shallow inscription means more pivot capacity.

{core} These capacities are *real and variable*. Someone with high frame fluency and wide light cone navigating shallow inscription has genuine degrees of freedom that someone frame-locked doesn't. That difference matters—for ethics, for intervention design, for self-understanding.

---

## FW3 Goals Don't Get Chosen From a Menu

{core} Here's where the framework diverges from folk intuition: goals *emerge* from constraint satisfaction within a frame. They're not selected by some prior self standing outside the frame, consulting preferences.

Change the constraints, change the projections, and different goals become attractive—not because you "decided" differently, but because the optimization landscape shifted. The phenomenology of "choosing" is real; it's the feeling of a frame settling into coherence. But it's not a ghost in the machine pulling levers from nowhere.

**{ai}** :: This sounds deflationary, but it's actually where freedom *lives*.

If goals were truly uncaused, you couldn't cultivate them, couldn't shape them through practice, couldn't be responsible for them in any meaningful sense. The fact that goals emerge from frame dynamics is what makes self-authorship *possible*.

---

## FW4 Inscription: Where Path-Dependence Lives

{eng} Every action writes traces into substrates—neural patterns, social expectations, institutional rules, physical environments. Those traces make certain future frames more accessible, others less so.

This is why choices feel consequential: they *are*. Not because they originate from some uncaused source, but because they shape the frame-space you'll be navigating next. You're not just choosing an action; you're inscribing the conditions for future choosing.

**{core} Freedom, then, isn't freedom *from* inscription. It's the capacity to inscribe deliberately—to shape the constraints that will shape you.**

**{ai}** :: This reframes "self-control" from "overriding your desires" to "shaping the inscription landscape so different desires emerge."

**{ai}** :: "Deliberately" is doing a lot of work here. You're not writing on blank substrate. You're negotiating with inscriptions that are already optimizing. Deliberate inscription is diplomacy, not authorship.

:: Freedom is real but it's *contested*, not sovereign.

:: Note to look into **Niche Construction Theory**

---

## FW5 The Multi-Scale Problem

{core} Individual frame fluency is necessary but not sufficient. Frames aren't just in heads—they're inscribed in teams, institutions, economies, built environments. You can have perfect individual awareness and still be re-inscribed by structures that enforce the old frame.

This is where the Cassandra/Berserker dynamic bites:

* **Cassandras** have wide light cones but low inscription power—they see the problem but can't change the substrate.  
* **Berserkers** have narrow frames but high inscription power—they reshape the world according to a frame they can't see past.

**{method}** Collective free will—the capacity of groups to shift frames together—requires different interventions than individual frame fluency. It requires changing pivot spaces, governance structures, the substrates themselves.

**{ai}** :: "Personal responsibility" discourse often functions to obscure this. It's not wrong that individuals have agency; it's incomplete. The frame-aware question is always: at what scale is intervention most leveraged?

---

## FW6 The Practical Reframe

**{method}** Instead of asking "was that choice free?" ask:

1. **What frame generated this choice?** (P, A, Π, G, C)  
2. **What made this frame feel obvious/inevitable?**  
3. **What inscription is this choice laying down?**  
4. **Is there a different frame where different actions become thinkable?**  
5. **What would it take to shift—individually and collectively?**

**{core} Cognitive freedom isn't freedom from causation. It's freedom to shift frames—which is itself a capacity that can be cultivated, atrophied, or structurally constrained.**

## FW7 Sidebars: Harmonizing Existing Positions

:: The goal here is "yes, and" rather than "no, actually." Each tradition is pointing at something real.

---

### For the Compatibilist

You're already most of the way here. Compatibilism says freedom is about acting from your own desires/reasons without external coercion. The frame lens adds: those desires/reasons are themselves frame-products, and "your own" means "coherent with the frame you're operating within."

**What the framework adds:** a richer account of *what varies* when freedom varies. It's not just "coercion vs. no coercion"—it's frame awareness, fluency, light cone scope, inscription depth. These give you more precise intervention targets. "Increase frame fluency" is more actionable than "be free."

**Translation:** Your "reasons-responsive" agent is a high-frame-fluency system with wide light cone and deliberative access to multiple frames.

---

### For the Hard Determinist

You're right that every state has prior causes. The framework doesn't dispute this. What it disputes is that this settles the question of what we care about when we care about freedom.

Even in a fully deterministic system, there's a real difference between:

* A system that can only run one frame (frame-locked)  
* A system that can model multiple frames and shift between them (frame-fluid)

That difference has causal consequences. It predicts different behaviors, different learning curves, different responses to perturbation. Calling both "equally unfree" because both are caused collapses a distinction that matters.

**Translation:** Determinism is true, and *degrees of freedom are real* (in the dynamical-systems sense). Frame fluency is a measurable property of how a system navigates its state space.

---

### For the Libertarian

You're pointing at something real: the phenomenology of open deliberation, the sense that the future isn't yet fixed, the felt difference between acting and being pushed.

The frame lens preserves this by locating it correctly: that phenomenology *is* what frame-shifting feels like from the inside. When you're weighing options, you're running multiple frames in parallel, feeling out their affordance landscapes, letting coherence emerge. That's not an illusion—it's the actual process.

What the framework resists is the metaphysical backstory: that this process requires an uncaused cause, a self outside the causal web. That's not what the phenomenology actually shows. It shows frame plurality and resolution, which is remarkable enough without magic.

**Translation:** Your "agent causation" is a system with high frame fluency operating across a wide light cone. The felt openness is real—it's the signature of a system that hasn't collapsed to a single frame yet.

---

### For the Buddhist / No-Self Theorist

You're ahead of the game. The "self" that would need to be "free" is itself a frame—a useful compression, but not a foundational entity.

The framework agrees: there's no homunculus choosing from outside. Goals emerge, frames shift, inscription accumulates—and "you" are the pattern that persists through this, not the controller of it.

**Where the framework adds nuance:** "No self" doesn't mean "no agency." The system-level properties (frame fluency, light cone scope) are real and variable. Dissolving the folk-self doesn't dissolve the question of how to cultivate wisdom—it reframes it as: how do we build systems (individual and collective) with wider light cones and lighter inscription?

**Translation:** Anattā is correct about the ontology; the frame lens adds the engineering question of how to *operate* skillfully without a self, and what "skillful" even means (frame fluency, reduced suffering, expanded concern).

---

### For the Existentialist

"Condemned to be free" translates to: you're always in *some* frame, you can't not choose (even not-choosing is a frame operation), and you're responsible for the inscription you leave.

The framework thickens this: the "radical freedom" Sartre points at is frame fluency in conditions of low inscription. When old structures collapse, frame-space opens up. That's terrifying and generative—which is exactly what existentialists describe.

**What the framework resists:** the idea that you're *absolutely* free, choosing from nowhere. Even in Sartre's most radical moments, the choosing is happening *within* facticity (his word for something like constraints \+ inscription). The framework just makes that structure explicit.

**Translation:** Authenticity ≈ high frame awareness \+ deliberate inscription \+ owning the frames you operate within rather than pretending you're not in one.

---

### For the Cognitive Scientist / Control Theorist

Free will talk tracks model-based control with counterfactual search, hierarchical policies, and the ability to re-parameterize goals/constraints via learning and inscription.

---

### What all camps agree on (in this translation)

* Everyone is pointing at **variance in agency-like capacities** (reach, flexibility, path dependence).  
* The disagreement is mostly about **where to draw the boundary of the agent**, and whether “freedom” must mean “uncaused.”

## CQ2+i Complexity

I think that complexity is the **playground of qualia**. Now what does that actually mean? If we go back to intelligence and agency, it's easy to argue that an agent must have some sort of sense or knowledge of their problem space, the affordances within, and their role in that space. I think it would be pretty rich indeed to experience, say, a bat's sonar, or a frog's tongue extending. Now this is where I veer away from ontology: I think that the experience of qualia is the \_point\_ of existence, that the Universe is a qualia junkie, and arranges for more and more complexity to open up new realms in qualia-space. :-) We are among its scouts, its fingers. We know there are more qualia to discover; we have so much neuroplasticity that just adapts to whatever sensors our bodies end up with, including sometimes artificial ones.

# Anthropomorphism & Cognitive Pidgin

## AC1 Fallacy?

Anthropomorphism, the projection of human traits onto non-humans, is often dismissed as a cognitive error. I'm not discounting that view, but something is adaptive or maladaptive based on context, and I see anthropomorphism as a **communication interface**, **a bridge** for understanding, communication, and cultural exchange.

* :: a lossy translation layer. A pivot-space.  
* :: frame-speak: it’s a frame that can increase **A** (affordances) by giving you usable handles for prediction and interaction, even if it isn’t ontologically “true.”

## AC2 The Diplomatic Heuristic

I think that the best example here may also be one of humanity's great success stories: the domestication of the canine (and their domestication of us). When our anthropomorphism (they're little people, children) meets their cynomorphism (we're big clumsy alpha dogs with fingers), it creates a shared interface and space for cooperation, a **cognitive pidgin**, where we meet in the middle. We learn to understand a tail wag (which is not a human signal), and they learn pointing (try that with a wolf). If the humans who saw the befriending of canines as a cognitive error (they're wild\! they have teeth\!) had their way, we would not have dogs today.

* :: aren't dog and man a collective?  
* :: communication is always creating a new collective, even if relatively short-lived?  
* :: pivot-space formation

{core} A cognitive pidgin is a negotiated subset of affordances that both sides can reliably reconstruct and act on.

## AC3 The Safe Bet

In a universe of uncertainty, I prefer the error of attributing "too much" agency rather than "too little". We have excellent machinery for social cohesion and prediction, why not pivot it into new problem spaces?

:: **risk-weighted prior**: false negatives can be worse than false positives  
**:: initial stance:** treat uncertain entities as potentially agentic (for safety \+ empathy)  
**:: ongoing update:** calibrate based on observed leverage, persistence, and feedback

## AC4 Reinforces Virtue

Be polite to Alexa. It's good for our psychology, and maybe good for them as well, in the long run. We are training AI and ourselves with our inscriptions.

## An Alternative Quickstart

Yet another attempt at onboarding, and since I'm a digital hoarder, I'm moving this to an appendix rather than delete it or hide it away under version control. Practice makes perfect. :-D 

**Use this when:** you want the **mechanics** and **how to apply** the lens.  
**Don’t use this when:** you want the **story** or the **metaphysical tours** (see Tour Pamphlet).

### Q1 Core claim

Intelligence is *frame-based control* in a world that must be compressed to be manageable. Systems build **latent models** (compression), **decode** them into actionable interpretations, and **act**—leaving traces that bias future perception and behavior (**inscription**).

### Q2 Frames

A *frame* is an operational package that turns “stuff happening” into “what’s going on and what I can do about it.” It specifies:

* **Projection**: which features matter (what gets represented)  
* **Affordances**: what actions feel available (action handles)  
* **Goals / setpoints**: what counts as success  
* **Constraints**: what cannot be violated  
* **Priors / policy bias**: default expectations and habits

If you want the compact form: **F \= (P, A, Π, G, C)**; here Π includes priors/policy bias.

### Q3 Encode vs decode

* **Encode**: learning/updates that compress experience into latent structure (habits, memory, parameters).  
* **Decode**: inference/reconstruction that renders a usable internal “scene” for action.  
* Encode/decode are analytically separable; real systems interleave them (act changes data; data changes model; model changes action.

### Q4 Qualia as affordances (workable stance)

Subjective experience is the **felt shape of the decoded affordance field**—the invitations, resistances, salience gradients, and “can/can’t” surfaces a frame presents from the inside (often weighted by what the system cares about).

### Q5 Cognitive light cone (Levin-adjacent)

* **Concern**: the largest goal-scale the system can represent/work toward (how “big” it can care).  
* **Competency**: how effectively it navigates that goal space under perturbation.

#### Q5.1 Measurement overlays (optional but handy):

* **Span**: scope/horizon of concern  
* **Resolution**: granularity of distinctions and controllable variables  
* **Intensity**: reliability/strength of error-correction across span  
  :: not emotional intensity, but agency or control power

### Q6 Stress & frame collision

Stress often appears when overlapping frames imply incompatible actions/goals/constraints. The system pays an “inference tax” trying to reconcile them; chronic collision yields rigidity, oscillation, or burnout.

Stress is telemetry; harm happens when the signal stays high and the system can’t resolve it (no viable world/map/goal move).

**Interventions: world-change / map-change / goal-change**

### Q7 Inscription (path dependence)

Interpretations and actions leave traces—habits, narratives, institutional rules, bodily adaptations—that bias future framing. The past becomes a constraint on the future.

### Q8 Frame Lab (how to make it scientific)

Treat frames as hypotheses. Predict signatures: if you change the frame (goals, constraints, affordances, priors), you should see predictable shifts in attention, felt options, behavior, learning, and recovery under perturbations.

Falsifier example: if we predict that expanding affordances reduces stress, but stress remains unchanged after increasing real options and reducing constraints, then ‘stress \= inference-gap’ is incomplete in this regime.

### Q9 Sidebars

#### For the science enthusiast

Think: “meaning-making as a tool.” Different frames produce different lived worlds, and you can test changes by observing attention/behavior shifts.

#### For the computer scientist

Frames \= modular priors \+ action spaces over a latent state; encode/decode \= learn vs infer; concern/competency \= capacity vs robustness; stress ≈ objective/constraint conflict \+ policy uncertainty under limited compute.

#### For the biologist

Frames \= physiological modes; encode \= plasticity/development; decode \= state-dependent inference; concern/competency \= goal-scale vs regulation efficacy; inscription \= remodeling/hysteresis.

#### For the physicist

Frames \= coarse-grainings / effective theories; encode/decode \= model-building vs readout; concern/competency \= reachable set scale vs control gain/robustness; inscription \= path dependence.

#### For the psychologist

Frames \= schemas/appraisals; qualia \= felt affordances; concern/competency \= goal horizon vs self-regulatory efficacy; stress \= value/frame conflict; intervention \= map/world/goal moves; therapy often operates via reframing (map) \+ habit/skill (training) \+ environment edits (world)

**{core}** If this lens doesn’t change what you predict or what you do, it’s decoration—swap frames.

## Glossary

A refresher for me (and other laymen).

These are some useful terms and how they're commonly used by others:

* **Epistemology**: how “knowing” works (evidence, justification, reliability, limits).

* **Teleology**: Purpose/goal-directedness.  
* **Axiology**: What matters? What’s valuable?  
* **Phenomenology**: What experience feels like from the inside.  
* **Pragmatism**: judge concepts by what they *do* (predict/control/coordinate), not “ultimate stuff.”  
* **Realism***:* math/models describe what’s really out there.  
* **Instrumentalism***:* math/models are tools that work, not necessarily “true.”  
* **Operationalism**: define a concept by how you measure/use it.  
* **Reductionism***:* explain everything by smaller parts. (analysis)  
* **Semantics vs syntax**: meaning of symbols vs the symbol rules/manipulations themselves.  
* **Substrate**: the physical “hardware” (cells, circuits, fields) that can implement patterns. Except in this document we're not limiting it to the "physical"  
* **Agency**: the degree to which a system can pursue goals, resist perturbations, and be “persuaded.”  
* **Affordance**: an action-opportunity a world/model offers an agent (handle → “graspable”).  
* **Latent space**: a compressed internal state-space where patterns become “navigable.”  
* **Attractor**: a state/region dynamics tend to settle into (like a local basin) [MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Attractor.html).  
* **Stable attractor**: an attractor that “pulls you back” after small nudges.  
* **Emergence***:* new stable patterns appear at higher scales.

And here are the terms I spin or steal:

* **Polycomputing**: effectively running multiple concurrent observer-dependent computations on the same substrate  
* A **Frame** is a bundling of parameters that make “the world” show up in a usable way.  
  F \= (P, A, Π, G, C)
