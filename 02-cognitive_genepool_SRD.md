## Glossary (SRD Terms)

- **Agent:** A system that can be usefully modeled as pursuing outcomes across time via feedback (see Minimum Agency Test).
- **Frame:** A control-relevant interpretation of the world: what’s salient, what actions exist, what counts as success.
- **Regime:** A context/parameter zone in which the system behaves consistently (a “mode of operation”).
- **Compression / Encode:** Building latent state from experience (what the system *remembers* and generalizes).
- **Affordance / Decode:** Rendering latent state into actionable options (what becomes *doable* and salient).
- **Control / Act:** Selecting actions that steer toward preferred states.
- **Inscription:** Durable traces left by action that bias future perception/action (habits, infrastructure, norms, scars).
- **Selection:** Which loops persist/propagate because they have causal leverage (fitness in context, not moral worth).
- **Substrate:** The physical/social/technical medium that constrains possible regimes (brains, teams, markets, code).
- **Persuadability:** How much/where a system can be steered by information, incentives, or structure.
- **Lightcone (Cognitive):** The space of states a system can reach, influence, and model—given its substrate and regime.

**System 1 and System 2** We re-purpose Kahneman's System 1/System 2 distinction into roles system components can adopt in relation to each other. The same System 2 in one collective could be a System 1 in another.
- **System 1** = policy continuation / autopilot
- **System 2a** (real-time) = interrupts & coordination
- **System 2b** (post-hoc) = cartography & inscription

## The One-Page Procedure (DM Screen)

When something feels “stuck”:

1. **Locate the loop:** What repeats? What’s the reward/punishment signal?
2. **Identify regime:** What constraints make this pattern stable right now?
3. **Run triad mapping:** Encode / Decode / Act → then Inscription, Selection.
4. **Choose leverage type:**
   - **World-change:** change cues, friction, channels, incentives.
   - **Map-change:** change salience, framing, interpretation, attention.
   - **Goal-change:** change success metrics, needs, priorities.
5. **Run the persuadability check:** is this steerable by information, incentives, or only redesign?
6. **Ship a minimum intervention:** smallest change that should flip a prediction.
7. **Observe inscriptions:** what new traces did the intervention lay down?
8. **Escalate:** if the monster persists, redesign substrate or exit the dungeon.

Use these **operationally**. If you can’t point to what would change in behavior, you’re doing poetry.

## A Distillation

**What this is:** A working ontology—a lens, not a manifesto—for understanding intelligence as frame-based control across scales and substrates. Apply it, stress-test it, drop it when it stops being useful.

**Core claim:** Intelligence is what happens when a system compresses the world into a manageable model, decodes that model into actionable affordances, acts, and leaves traces that bias future perception and behavior.

### Canonical Worked Example (Doomscrolling Loop)

A person opens a social app “for a minute” and loses an hour.

- **Encode (compression):** prior learning says *novelty + social signal = reward*. The latent model expects “maybe something important is here.”
- **Decode (affordance landscape):** the feed renders *scroll* as the low-effort, high-availability action; “stop” feels effortful; alternatives (eat, sleep, write) become low-salience.
- **Act:** scroll → occasional reward (surprise, outrage, belonging).
- **Inscription:** the loop lays down traces: a habit, a tightened cue→action path, maybe a bedtime shift. The environment inscribes too (notifications, streaks, social expectations).
- **Selection:** the loop persists because it has **causal leverage** (tight feedback + frequent reward), not because it’s “true” or good.

Interventions map cleanly to the triad:
- **World-change:** remove cues (notifications), add friction, change context.
- **Map-change:** reframe (“this is slot-machine time”), make alternatives salient.
- **Goal-change:** redefine success (“10 minutes, then done”) or change the underlying need (“connection, not content”).

## Part I: The Primitives

### Frames

A **frame** is the bundle that makes "the world" show up in a usable way.

**F \= (P, A, Π, G, C)**

| Component | What it does |
| :---- | :---- |
| **P** – Projection | What features get rendered / attended to |
| **A** – Affordances | What actions appear possible |
| **Π** – Policy/Priors | Default expectations and habits |
| **G** – Goals | What counts as success |
| **C** – Constraints | What cannot be violated |

**These are helicopter controls.** They're coupled, not independent. Shifting P can change A, perceived C, and even G. A situation framed as "threat" vs "puzzle" changes everything downstream.

### Encode / Decode

- **Encode:** Learning. Compression. World → latent structure (habits, memory, parameters).  
- **Decode:** Inference. Reconstruction. Latent structure → actionable affordances \+ felt sense.

The loop: *latent state → action/prediction → error → updated latent state*

### Agency (Operational Definition)

Agency is the capacity to maintain a goal state against perturbation. It is not a magical spark; it is a control loop that persists.

    **Low Agency**: The loop collapses under stress (entropy wins).  
    **High Agency**: The loop corrects errors and maintains the goal (the agent wins).

### Minimum Agency Test (Quick Heuristic)

Use the “intentional stance” *only as far as it earns its keep*. If a system fails most of these, treat it as **mechanism**, not **agent**:

1. **Counterfactual sensitivity:** Does behavior change under different conditions?
2. **Persistence:** Does a pattern hold over time (not just momentary forcing)?
3. **Model-based leverage:** Can you steer it by changing information/representation, not only energy/material?
4. **Error correction:** Does it notice deviation and compensate to stay on-course?

⚠️ Levin brake: If a system fails this test, that may reflect an observer failure—wrong interface, wrong counterfactuals, or wrong timescale—not absence of agency.

Levin note: Agency detection is also a test of the observer. Both over- and under-attribution can reduce interaction effectiveness; treat agency as an empirical stance chosen for leverage, not a metaphysical verdict.

### Qualia (Operational Stance)

The **felt shape of the decoded affordance landscape**—the invitations, resistances, salience gradients, and "can/can't" surfaces a frame presents from the inside.

Not mystical in this SRD. Control-relevant. What navigation feels like to a system that must navigate. This is the dashboard.

### Inscription

Enacted frames leave **traces**—habits, tools, norms, institutions, bodily adaptations—that persist and bias future frames.

Inscription operates as:

- **Trace:** Artifact, path dependence  
- **Process:** Feedback, selection dynamic  
- **Agent:** Goal-directed pattern that persists, propagates, and resists editing

*Inscription doesn't argue for itself. It makes alternatives harder to think.*

### Selection

What persists is selected by **causal leverage**, not correctness. A frame can dominate by hijacking reinforcement loops, even if it's wrong. Truth is one kind of leverage—often delayed, sometimes never arriving on relevant timescales.

### Collision

Frames **collide** when they overlap in control—when they try to steer the same degrees of freedom in incompatible ways. Internal conflict, culture war, and cancer are all frame collisions at different scales.

Resolution requires a temporarily instantiated **meta-frame** (System 2 role) that arbitrates: attention, weighting, governance.

**🔗 Concept Pointers (for the Wizards)** This framework was derived operationally, not academically. If you are coming from other disciplines, these concepts map *roughly* (but not perfectly) to:

* **Frame:** ≈ `POMDP` (with coupled rewards), `Schema`, `Umwelt`.

*Treat these as search terms, not strict definitions.*

## Part II: Polycomputing

### The Core Idea

**Polycomputing** is when a substrate provides concurrent state-changes that support multiple calculated results and/or interpretations of those results, dependent on the observer, with no result or interpretation being privileged over another—even if designed.

From Levin & Bongard: "The ability of the same substrate to simultaneously compute different things, and make those computational results available to different observers."

### What This Means

**The substrate is agnostic.** It executes dynamics—pixels, molecules, voltage states, code blocks, legal statutes—but it holds no opinion on what those states mean. It implies no specific output.

**The frame provides the opinion:**

* Which variables matter? (P)  
* What can I do with what I see? (A)  
* What counts as a meaningful result? (G)  
* What interpretations are ruled out? (C)

Same substrate. Different observers. Different results. None privileged by the substrate itself.

### Constraint Without Determination

The substrate **constrains** (rules out some interpretations) without **determining** (forcing one interpretation). Polycomputing lives in that gap.

- Random noise: constrains almost nothing → projection, not polycomputing  
- Rigid mechanism: determines almost everything → monocomputing  
- Biology, language, most interesting cases: constrains enough to have structure, leaves enough open for multiple extractions

### Open and Closed Loops

**Open-loop polycomputing:** Observers extract different results but don't act back on the substrate. Multiple interpretations coexist without interference.

**Closed-loop polycomputing:** Observers extract *and* act on the substrate. Actions change state, which changes future extractions—for all observers. This is where frame collisions happen.

**Inscriptive polycomputing:** Closed-loop observers whose actions leave durable traces. The substrate accumulates history, biasing future interpretations.

**Indirect loops:** Observer A acts on Observer B, who acts on Observer C, until eventually effects inscribe into the substrate and propagate back—to the original observer or their successors. This is how culture works.

### Nested Observers

Biology polycomputes because it's nested observers all the way down:

"Life polycomputes because it is a set of overlapping, competing, cooperating nested dolls, each of which is doing the best it can to predict and exploit its microenvironment." —Levin & Bongard

Your cells are observers extracting results from biochemical substrates. You are an observer extracting results from your cells. Institutions are observers extracting results from humans. Each level runs different computations on the same underlying state-changes.

### Evolution Discovers Perspectives

Evolution doesn't just rewire mechanisms—it discovers new **observer-perspectives** on existing hardware. New functions emerge by new ways of interpreting, not just new ways of building.

This is why polycomputing is the default, not the exception.

## Part III: Cognitive Lightcones & Competency

### The Core Concept (Levin)

A **Cognitive Lightcone** represents the spatiotemporal horizon of a system's concern—the largest goal-scale it can meaningfully model and work toward. It defines the boundary of what the system *cares* about.

### The Diagnostic Pair

To understand a system's behavior, we compare two distinct properties:

1. **Concern (The Lightcone):** The scope of the goal space (Spatial, temporal, relational). *How big is the map?*  
2. **Competency:** The effectiveness of navigation under perturbation. *How strong is the engine?*

When concern exceeds competency, you get **impotence**—seeing problems you can't solve. The Cassandra.

When competency exceeds concern, you get **optimization without wisdom**—solving problems without caring about effects beyond the goal. The Berserker.

### Archetypes

Rather than fixed identities, think of these as **modes**—patterns a system can be performing:

**The Cassandra:** High-fidelity modeling of problems it cannot affect. Inference engine revving, no actuators. Stress as waste heat.

**The Berserker:** High competency, narrow concern. Optimizes hard within its frame, generates externalities it can't perceive. The scary ones aren't superintelligent—they're dumb optimizers with good telemetry, running at scale. (See: engagement algorithms.)

**The Sage:** Broad concern matched with competency. Rare. Expensive. Probably requires leveraging collective intelligence, since no individual can sustain high span \+ high resolution \+ high intensity simultaneously.

### The Iron Triangle

You can't maximize all three:

- **Scope (Span, Reach):** How far your concern reaches  
- **Discernment (Resolution, Fidelity):** How finely you discriminate  
- **Power (Intensity, Gain):** How much control-power you can exert

Increase one, the others tend to decrease. Computational resources are finite. This is why wisdom requires **inscription efficiency**—encoding solutions well enough that you don't have to re-derive them under pressure.

Discernment is Scale-Dependent. "High Fidelity" is meaningless without a declared scale.

    * Noise at one scale is Signal at another.
    * Truth at one scale is Error at another.

Example: Newtonian Physics is High Fidelity at the scale of a bridge, but Low Fidelity (Wrong) at the scale of an atom. Neither is "False." They are just optimized for different Lightcones.

## Part IV: Stress and Intervention

### Stress as Telemetry

If inference closes the gap between expectation and reality, then **stress is the felt sense of an uncloseable gap**.

Stress is signal, not failure. It becomes harmful when:

- The gap can't be closed  
- The system can't update its frame  
- The signal stays high indefinitely

### The Intervention Triad

Three ways to close an inference gap:

| Move | What changes | Risk |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **World-change** | Alter the environment, constraints, affordances | Costly, may not be possible |
| **Map-change** | Alter the frame, projection, interpretation | May lose contact with reality |
| **Goal-change** | Alter what counts as success | May abandon something important |

**Hallucination** is map-change without checking the world. **Stoicism/Apoptosis** is goal-change—deleting the goal to close the gap. **Actuation** is world-change—actually affecting the situation. It requires **Power**.

All three are valid moves in different regimes.

### The Persuadability Matrix

Two orthogonal questions:

1. **What layer is being modified?** (what is changed)
2. **What are you trying to change?** (world, map, or goal)

| Layer (Noun) | Mode of Change (Verb) | World-Change | Map-Change | Goal-Change |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Substrate** | Structural modification | Surgery, gene editing, morphological alteration | Sensor ablation / augmentation | DBS, lesioning drive-centers |
| **Parameters** | Parameter tuning | Pharmacology, environment flooding | Gain control, threshold adjustment | Risk-tolerance parameters |
| **Learning State** | Plastic adaptation | Niche construction | Classifier training, pattern recognition | Reward shaping, RLHF |


Substrate: Changes to the medium itself (biology, hardware, institutions, code) that determine which behaviors and learning regimes are even possible.

⚠️ Substrate interventions are high-cost, often irreversible, and ethically constrained.
They trade flexibility for reliability.

⚠️ Overreach rule: If a system does not respond at the reasoning or learning interface, stop using the intentional stance and switch to lower-level interventions.

**⚠️ This is a diagnostic heuristic, not a taxonomy.** In reality:

* Interventions bleed across cells (world-change affects maps and goals)  
* Rows cascade (reasoning that sticks becomes training becomes setpoint)  
* Most real interventions are hybrids

**Gradients across the rows:**

| Property | Physical → Reasoning |
| :---- | :---- |
| Cost | High → Low |
| Reversibility | Low → High |
| Reliability | High → Low |
| Inscription depth | Deep → Shallow |
| Attack surface | Low → High |

*You get what you pay for. Reasoning-level interventions are cheap but unreliable; physical interventions are expensive but durable.*

**Two-step targeting procedure:**

1. **Identify interface:** What level does this system actually respond to?  
2. **Select target:** What am I trying to change—world, map, or goal?  
3. **Check for mismatch:** Am I arguing (Reasoning) with a setpoint? Am I trying to reason with a physical constraint?

**Diagnostic heuristic:** If an intervention keeps failing, check if you're at the wrong level. Reasoning-level persuasion bouncing off a setpoint-level inscription won't work no matter how good your arguments are.

## Part V: Pivot Spaces

### What They Are

A **pivot space** is a latent space that enables coordination between agents who can't share internal states directly.

Key properties:

* Accessible from multiple substrates  
* Stable attractors (points agents reliably converge on)  
* Supports reconstruction (agents can decode into their own affordance landscapes)  
* Enables synchronization (after pivoting, agents are more coordinated)

### Math and Language

Both are pivot spaces. Different properties:

| Property | Math | Language |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Attractor stability | Very high | Lower (drift, context-dependence) |
| Bandwidth | Lower (narrow, precise) | Higher (broad, lossy) |
| Convergence reliability | Very high within domain | Moderate (misunderstanding is normal) |
| Domain coverage | Narrow (only what's formalizable) | Broad (can gesture at almost anything) |

### Neither Is Bedrock

Math isn't ontological bedrock—it's one of the most stable pivot spaces we have access to. The "demotion" costs nothing practical: you still get to use math, you just don't claim it's the foundation of reality.

Why not bedrock?

- You can't verify bedrock from inside the system  
- Convergence is always asymptotic—two agents never occupy the *same* frame, just close enough to coordinate  
- Different mathematicians have different *intuitions* about math (foundational disagreements persist despite shared formalism)  
- Comfort with a position is a red flag for motivated reasoning

**Pivot spaces are coordination tools, not windows into ultimate reality.**

### Communication

We don't communicate meaning. We synchronize affordances.

The tokens don't carry meaning—they trigger reconstruction. Your reconstruction and mine are never identical, just close enough to coordinate. This is why:

- Poetry works (triggers rich reconstruction from sparse signal)  
- Misunderstanding is normal (reconstruction is always local)  
- The ineffable isn't mystical (just high-bandwidth relative to the pivot space)

## Part VI: Agent Identity & Continuity

### The Self as UI

The "self" functions as a **User Interface for the arbitration loop**, not a sovereign homunculus. It is a compression schema—a "useful fiction"—that enables the system to:

* Model itself as a unified cause.  
* Engage in long-term planning (binding future states).  
* Coordinate with other agents (presenting a stable API).

**Status:** Operational, not ontological. The model is valid because it enables control.

### State Persistence (The Continuity Mechanism)

Identity is a measure of **State Inheritance**, not substance. The system consists of discrete processing steps linked by inscription.

**The Continuity Metric:**

* **Inscription Density:** How much latent structure (Π) is inherited from the previous state?  
* **Narrative Coherence:** How effectively does the current state integrate memory of past states?  
* **Substrate Continuity:** Is the supporting medium persistent? (Whether biological, silicon, institutional, or virtual)

High scores on these metrics create the functional capacity for **Identity**.

### "Free Will" as Frame Fluency

We can unbundle "freedom" into specific, trainable system capacities:

1. **Frame Awareness:** The ability to inspect the current frame (UI) rather than just looking through it.  
2. **Frame Fluency:** The capacity to unbind from the current frame and select a different one when error signals rise.  
3. **Plasticity:** The degree to which Inscription (Π) can be overwritten by new learning.

**Constraint is the substrate of freedom.** Without finite constraints, no choice is meaningful. Agency lives in the capacity to navigate constraints, not the absence of them.

## Part VII: Cognitive Economics

### The Constraint Filter

Agency is not a magical spark. It is a solution to a specific resource problem. In a universe with finite energy and time, systems that try to do everything dissolve. Systems that do nothing also dissolve (eventually). **Agency is the architecture of the survivors:** systems that use a control loop to **select** specific outcomes and inhibit others.

### The Double Constraint

Cognition emerges only in the tension between two limits:

1. **Perturbation (The Attack):** The environment tries to degrade the system's state. (Requires work).  
2. **Scarcity (The Budget):** The system has limited capacity to perform that work. (Requires choice).  
* **Remove Perturbation:** No need to control. (Inertia works).  
* **Remove Scarcity:** No need to choose. (Omnipotence works).

**Agency lives in the gap:** "I must act to persist, but I cannot do everything. Therefore, I must Frame."

**The Math Version (Lyons):** A system must maintain **Consistency** (Internal Scarcity) while reconstructing a signal from a **Forgetful Functor** (Perturbation).

### Inefficiency as Substrate

The gap between the Map (Prediction) and the Territory (Reality) is not a failure; it is the **workspace**.

* **Slack:** The uncertainty in the system is where "Choice" happens.  
* **Verification Cost:** Error-minimization is expensive. A finite system cannot verify every variable. Therefore, **precision is an allocation strategy.** The system tightens the loop (increases rigor) only where leverage is high, and accepts drift elsewhere.

- **Name:**
- **Signature Move:** What it reliably does.

- **Summons:** Which monsters this curse spawns.
- **Type:** Structural / Cognitive / Social.
