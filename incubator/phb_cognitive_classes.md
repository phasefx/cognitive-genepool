# PHB: The Cognitive Party

## This Is Not a Game

The D&D language is here for two reasons:

1. **Vibes:** It makes self-examination feel less clinical and more approachable.
2. **Leverage:** Metaphors compress patterns into handles. "Summon the Paladin" is easier to act on than "increase commitment via executive function." The intentional stance lets you negotiate with processes as if they have goals.

What it's **not** for:

- Leveling up your character
- Optimizing your party composition  
- Collecting achievements
- Identifying as a "type"

**You are not a Wizard. You are not a Paladin.** You are a system that sometimes processes like a Wizard, sometimes like a Paladin. The classes are *verbs*, not nouns. Modes you can notice and shift, not identities to cultivate.

If you find yourself min-maxing this sheet, the Artificer has captured you.

## The Classes

These are stabilized cognitive modes — ways of processing reality that work well for specific types of challenges. Everyone has access to all of them. The question is which one is *currently driving* and whether that's appropriate for the situation.

### The Wizard (Projection)
**Mode:** Map-making. Modeling, abstracting, system-building.

**Strength:** Sees patterns others miss. Builds coherent models. Finds the structure.

**Signature Cost:** May be seen as "pedantic," "cold," or "out of touch." Can get lost in the map and forget the territory.

**Under Stress Becomes:** The Reboot Loop — re-analyzing the foundation to avoid the finish line.

---

### The Ranger (Affordance)
**Mode:** Map-contact. Intuition, fast-reads, signals, "touching grass."

**Strength:** Navigates novel terrain quickly. Reads the room. Gets external signal.

**Signature Cost:** May be unable to explain or justify the gut read. Can look like "just vibes."

**Under Stress Becomes:** The Cassandra — sees the signal but has no lever to pull.

---

### The Bard (Frame)
**Mode:** Lens-shifting. Narrating, reframing, persuasion, vibes.

**Strength:** Can shift perspectives — yours and others'. Finds the story that unsticks.

**Signature Cost:** May be seen as "slippery," "theatrical," or unserious. Can mistake eloquence for truth.

**Under Stress Becomes:** The Captured Narrator — defending a story instead of looking at the data.

---

### The Artificer (Inscription)
**Mode:** Scaffold-building. Tools, systems, habit-engines.

**Strength:** Extends capacity. Offloads cognition. Turns effort into autopilot.

**Signature Cost:** May be accused of "over-engineering." Tools can ossify and become prisons.

**Under Stress Becomes:** The Ossified Scaffold — fighting for the tool/process, not the outcome.

---

### The Paladin (Goals/Constraints)
**Mode:** Line-holding. Commitment, terminal values, shipping.

**Strength:** Follows through. Resists drift. Actually finishes things.

**Signature Cost:** May be called "inflexible," "mean," or "unfun." Can rigidify and moralize.

**Under Stress Becomes:** The Berserker — optimizing a narrow target, ignoring collateral damage.

---

### The Rogue (Priors)
**Mode:** Edge-finding. Counterfactuals, exploits, breaking habits. "What if this is wrong?"

**Strength:** Sees what others ignore. Questions the obvious. Finds the gaming.

**Signature Cost:** May be seen as "difficult," "paranoid," or "contrarian." Can over-index on hidden explanations.

**Under Stress Becomes:** The Paranoid Generator — finding "secret patterns" that justify fear.

## Mapping Classes to Primitives

Each class corresponds to a component of the Frame tuple **F = (P, A, Π, G, C)**:

| Class | Primary Primitive | The "Meta-Move" |
|:------|:------------------|:----------------|
| **Wizard** | P (Projection) | Adjusts the resolution of the model |
| **Ranger** | A (Affordances) | Spots hidden paths in the current terrain |
| **Bard** | F (The Frame itself) | Switches the entire lens |
| **Artificer** | I (Inscription) | Builds tools that turn effort into autopilot |
| **Paladin** | G/C (Goals/Constraints) | Holds the line on what must/must not happen |
| **Rogue** | Π (Priors/Policy) | Breaks habits to test counterfactuals |

## The Hero-to-Monster Pipeline

When a class is under pressure and *refuses to swap*, it degrades into a monster. This is the central diagnostic:

| Class | Stress Mode | Monster | The Tell |
|:------|:------------|:--------|:---------|
| Wizard | Infinite re-analysis | **Reboot Loop** | Rebuilding the foundation to avoid finishing |
| Ranger | Signal overload | **Cassandra** | Seeing the problem, having no lever |
| Bard | Self-sealing narrative | **Captured Narrator** | Defending story over data |
| Artificer | Infrastructure lock | **Ossified Scaffold** | Fighting for tool, not outcome |
| Paladin | Goal rigidity | **Berserker** | Narrow optimization, collateral damage |
| Rogue | Edge-case paranoia | **Paranoid Generator** | Secret patterns justifying fear |

**The key insight:** The class that summoned the monster cannot defeat it. You must multi-class — bring a different mode to the table.

## Multi-Classing (The Intervention)

When you diagnose a monster, the fix is rarely "try harder with the same mode." It's *changing the party composition.*

### Strategic Pairings

**To break a Reboot Loop:**
- **Paladin:** Pick a finish line you can't renegotiate. Write it down. Tell someone.
- **Ranger:** Get one external signal in the next 10 minutes.

**To break a Captured Narrator:**
- **Ranger:** Touch grass. Get an error signal from outside the narrative.
- **Wizard:** Build one falsifiable prediction. Write down what would prove you wrong.

**To break an Ossified Scaffold:**
- **Rogue:** Ask "what would we do if this tool didn't exist?"
- **Bard:** Tell a new story about why the old scaffold served its purpose and can be retired.

**To break a Metric Maximizer / Berserker:**
- **Rogue:** Find where the metric is being gamed. Name the gap between metric and goal.
- **Paladin:** Re-anchor to the terminal goal. Say it out loud.

## The Captured Narrator as Final Boss

The Captured Narrator is the most dangerous monster because it wears the costumes of other classes:

- It looks like the **Wizard** (using logic)
- It sounds like the **Bard** (telling a compelling story)
- It acts like the **Paladin** (claiming to defend values)

**The tell:** Unlike a true class, the Captured Narrator *cannot multi-class*. It is stuck in one defense. A real Wizard can stop being a Wizard to look at a problem like a Rogue. The Captured Narrator must keep narrating.

## The Bench

**Which class do you avoid or rarely use?**

This is often where monsters hide. If you never use the Rogue, you have blind spots. If you never use the Paladin, you have drift. If you never use the Ranger, you have map-fetishism.

The "benched" class represents a mode you're not allowed to access — either by habit, fear, or identity. Unbenching it is often the highest-leverage intervention.
