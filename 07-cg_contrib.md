# The Cognitive Genepool

## Contributor Guide

*How to add creatures without summoning gods*

---

## 0. What You Are Contributing To (Read This First)

**This is not a canon.**
It is a **field kit** and a **genepool**.

* You are not transmitting truth.
* You are seeding patterns.
* Mutation is expected.
* Misunderstandings are allowed.
* Improvements via divergence are success.

If your contribution:

* helps someone *notice a pattern faster*, or
* helps someone *choose a better intervention*, or
* prevents a predictable failure mode,

…it belongs here.

If it mainly:

* defends a worldview,
* settles metaphysics,
* or proves you’re right,

it probably doesn’t.

---

## 1. Lanes: Where Your Contribution Lives

Every contribution must clearly live in **one primary lane**.
You may reference others, but don’t blur them.

### The Lanes

* **Mainline** — actionable tools, diagnostics, monsters, spells
* **Method** — how we know if something works (Frame Lab, falsifiers)
* **Metaphor** — intuition pumps, stories, vibes (explicitly labeled)
* **Meta / Arcana** — self-critique, governance, speculative bets

**Rule:**
If a reader can’t tell which lane they’re in, the contribution isn’t ready.

**Why this exists:**
Prevents rigor cosplay, metaphysical bleed, and “this framework explains everything” failure.

---

## 2. What “Done” Means Here (Shipping Threshold)

A contribution is **ready** when:

* It changes what a reader would **notice**, **predict**, or **do**
* It survives one honest attempt to break it
* It doesn’t require accepting hidden assumptions
* It can be *dropped* without the framework collapsing

You do **not** need:

* completeness
* elegance
* formal proof
* academic defensibility

You **do** need:

* clarity
* humility
* an exit ramp

---

## 3. Contribution Types (Pick One)

### A. Monster Manual Entries

Patterns of intelligence gone sideways.

Must include:

* a **one-sentence pattern**
* a **tell**
* a **why it matters**
* at least one **intervention that backfires**
* at least one **real example**

Optional:

* lair actions
* control ecology
* observables / falsifiers

> If the Combat Card isn’t good, the monster isn’t real yet.

---

### B. Cognitive Spells / Tools

Moves a person or group can actually try.

Must include:

* when to use it
* when *not* to use it
* how it fails
* a 24–72h test

---

### C. Scaffolds

Tools that extend capacity *without capturing*.

Must include:

* what it offloads
* how it ossifies
* when to throw it away

---

### D. DMG / Systems Tools

Intervention design, targeting, stress-testing.

Must include:

* regime assumptions
* where it backfires
* what level it actually operates at

---

### E. Arcana / Deep Lore

Speculative, metaphysical, or unresolved material.

Must include:

* explicit **speculation** label
* why it’s interesting
* how it could be wrong
* why it’s *optional*

---

## 4. Non-Negotiable Rules (Anti-Bullshit)

### Rule 1: Patterns Are Verbs, Not Identities

You are describing **behaviors under conditions**, not types of people.

> “This system is performing X,” not “this system *is* X.”

---

### Rule 2: Naming Is Not Exculpation

Explaining a mechanism does **not** remove responsibility.

Do not use monsters as:

* moral shields
* excuses
* status weapons

---

### Rule 3: The Framework Is Also a Monster

If your contribution:

* explains every outcome,
* resists falsification,
* or cannot be dropped,

it’s eating the dungeon.

Add a failure mode or cut it.

---

### Rule 4: No Hidden Ontology

If something only works *if the reader believes X is fundamentally true*, say so.

Better yet:
rewrite it so it works **instrumentally**, not metaphysically.

---

## 5. Falsifiers & Friction (Lightweight, Not Legalistic)

You don’t need formal proofs.
You *do* need a way to be wrong.

Acceptable falsifiers:

* “If X doesn’t happen, this diagnosis is wrong”
* “If intervention Y doesn’t move behavior, abandon this”
* “If this only works in toy examples, demote it to Arcana”

If you can’t imagine how you’d know it failed, pause.

---

## 6. The Sledgehammer Alternative (Required Self-Check)

Before submitting, ask:

> “What’s the simpler, less flattering explanation for what I’m describing?”

If that explanation:

* predicts the same behavior,
* suggests the same intervention,

…your contribution may be decorative.

That’s okay.
But it belongs in **Metaphor** or **Arcana**, not Mainline.

---

## 7. How to Disagree (and Improve the Pool)

Disagreement is encouraged.

Good critique:

* shows what the entry fails to predict
* offers a better pattern or boundary
* preserves the original as a variant if useful

Bad critique:

* argues ontology
* argues intent
* argues tone without leverage

Remember:
**Forking is allowed.**
Variants are healthy.

---

## 8. Capture Warnings (For Contributors)

You may be captured if:

* you feel compelled to defend your entry
* criticism feels like an attack
* you keep adding nuance instead of adding exits
* you’re more invested in coherence than usefulness

If that happens:

* take a break
* demote the entry
* or add a Meta-Note admitting uncertainty

That’s not failure.
That’s immune response.

---

## 9. Final Reminder

You are not writing scripture.
You are adding organisms to an ecosystem.

Some will die.
Some will mutate.
A few will thrive in places you didn’t expect.

That’s the point.
