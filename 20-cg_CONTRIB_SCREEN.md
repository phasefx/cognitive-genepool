## What a Contributor Screen *is* (in this project)

Not documentation.
Not policy.
Not vibes.

A **Contributor Screen** is:

> A compact, always-visible set of reminders that keep contributors from accidentally summoning monsters, dogma, or bureaucracy.

Think:

* DM Screen → protects players
* **Contributor Screen → protects the genepool**

---

## Physical / Conceptual Form

Design it so it works in *any* of these forms:

* a **tri-fold screen** (classic D&D)
* a **single PDF / image** pinned in a repo
* a **README section** you scroll past constantly
* a **Notion / Obsidian side panel**

### Design constraint

Each panel answers **one question contributors ask while mid-flow**.

---

## Proposed Contributor Screen (4 Panels)

### 🟦 PANEL 1 — Lanes & Scope

**“Where does this belong?”**

**Lanes (pick one):**

* Mainline → tools, monsters, spells
* Method → falsifiers, Frame Lab
* Metaphor → intuition pumps
* Arcana → speculative / dangerous / fun

**Rules:**

* One primary lane per contribution
* If it fits everywhere, it fits nowhere
* Mixed lanes must be labeled

**Red Flag:**

> “This feels true everywhere.”

---

### 🟥 PANEL 2 — Pattern Discipline

**“Is this actually a monster/tool?”**

**For Monsters:**

* Recurs across contexts
* Misdiagnosed in the same way
* Common fixes backfire
* Describable as a *verb*

**For Tools:**

* Changes behavior within a week
* Has a clear *when not to use*
* Fails in predictable ways

**Red Flag:**

> “This just explains things better.”

---

### 🟨 PANEL 3 — Anti-Capture & Falsifiers

**“How could this be wrong?”**

* What would disconfirm this?
* When should this be benched?
* What’s the sledgehammer explanation?
* Where does this get demoted if it fails?

**If you can’t answer:**
→ Arcana, Metaphor, or scrap

**Red Flag:**

> “Criticism feels like an attack.”

---

### 🟩 PANEL 4 — Shipping & Hygiene

**“Is this ready to release?”**

* Good enough > complete
* At least one backfire noted
* At least one exit ramp
* You’re okay if it mutates

**Final test:**

> Would removing this make the project healthier?

**Red Flag:**

> “I just need to polish it more.”

---

## What makes this *especially* good governance

### 1. It’s **ambient**

It doesn’t tell people what to think — it shapes *what they notice while working*.

### 2. It’s **status-safe**

No one has to say “you’re wrong.”
The screen does it quietly.

### 3. It scales

* Solo contributors → self-check
* Maintainers → shared reference
* Disagreements → point to a panel, not a person

### 4. It reinforces your deepest claims

* Frames are tools
* Patterns beat explanations
* Capture is the real enemy
* Selection > correctness

---

## How it should be referenced (important)

Never say:

> “You violated rule X.”

Always say:

> “This might be a Panel 2 issue,”
> or
> “Feels like a Panel 3 red flag.”

That language keeps things collaborative and non-carceral.
