## 1\. Appendices & Reference

### Curses

Monsters are stable pathological attractors.
Curses are conditions that bias systems toward those attractors.

Mnemonic:
    Disease vs risk factor
    Process vs field

Structural curses: Scarcity, Fragmentation, Lock-in
Cognitive curses: Physics Leakage, False Bedrock
Social curses: External Pressure, Audience Capture

### ☠️ Curse: Scarcity

**Looks like:**  
“We don’t have time”  
“There’s no slack for this”  
“Just ship it”

**Actually is:**  
A compression of time and resources that collapses option space

**Common triggers:**
- Deadlines
- Budget cuts
- Crisis framing
- Continuous partial emergencies

**Typical damage:**
- Shortened horizons  
- Suppressed error correction  
- Amplification of proxy-driven behavior

**Escape condition:**
- Inject slack (time, budget, attention)  
- Reduce simultaneous demands  
- Explicitly extend horizons

## ☠️ Curse Cluster: Metaphor & Model Pathologies

These curses distort interpretation and intervention by importing structure where it does not belong. They do not cause behavior directly; they bias which behaviors become stable.

### ☠️ Curse: Physics Leakage

**Looks like:**  
Rigor, inevitability, “hard constraints”

**Actually is:**  
A metaphor silently promoted to a causal model outside its native domain

**Common triggers:**
- Geometry metaphors (cones, gradients, surfaces)
- Optimization language
- “Law-like” claims borrowed from physics or math

**Typical damage:**
- False impossibility claims  
- Spurious tradeoffs  
- Premature closure

**Escape condition:**
- Demote the metaphor  
- Replace geometry with descriptive axes  
- Ask: *What breaks if this is only a diagram?*

### ☠️ Curse: False Bedrock

**Looks like:**  
“Everything rests on X”  
“Without this, nothing makes sense”

**Actually is:**  
A locally useful pivot mistaken for ontological foundation

**Common triggers:**
- Math-as-reality
- Incentives-as-laws
- Evolution-as-explanation-for-everything

**Typical damage:**
- Immunity to counterexamples  
- Moralization of disagreement  
- Collapse of plural lenses

**Escape condition:**
- Ask: *What work is this doing locally?*  
- Replace “fundamental” with “highly stable attractor”

### ☠️ Curse: Conservation Fallacy

**Looks like:**  
“If you gain X, you must lose Y”  
“There’s no free lunch”

**Actually is:**  
Assuming conserved quantities where none exist

**Common triggers:**
- Zero-sum thinking
- Energy metaphors applied to meaning, attention, or coordination

**Typical damage:**
- Missed positive-sum interventions  
- Artificial scarcity  
- Overconfidence in tradeoff narratives

**Escape condition:**
- Ask: *What exactly is being conserved, where, and why?*  
- Check whether the “loss” is a real cost or just unfamiliarity

##### Q1 Core claim

Intelligence is *frame-based control* in a world that must be compressed to be manageable. Systems build **latent models** (compression), **decode** them into actionable interpretations, and **act**—leaving traces that bias future perception and behavior (**inscription**).

##### Q2 Frames

A *frame* is an operational package that turns “stuff happening” into “what’s going on and what I can do about it.” It specifies:

* **Projection**: which features matter (what gets represented)  
* **Affordances**: what actions feel available (action handles)  
* **Goals / setpoints**: what counts as success  
* **Constraints**: what cannot be violated  
* **Priors / policy bias**: default expectations and habits

If you want the compact form: **F \= (P, A, Π, G, C)**; here Π includes priors/policy bias.

##### Q3 Encode vs decode

* **Encode**: learning/updates that compress experience into latent structure (habits, memory, parameters).  
* **Decode**: inference/reconstruction that renders a usable internal “scene” for action.  
* Encode/decode are analytically separable; real systems interleave them (act changes data; data changes model; model changes action.

##### Q4 Qualia as affordances (workable stance)

Subjective experience is the **felt shape of the decoded affordance field**—the invitations, resistances, salience gradients, and “can/can’t” surfaces a frame presents from the inside (often weighted by what the system cares about).

##### Q5 Cognitive light cone (Levin-adjacent)

* **Concern**: the largest goal-scale the system can represent/work toward (how “big” it can care).  
* **Competency**: how effectively it navigates that goal space under perturbation.

###### Q5.1 Measurement overlays (optional but handy):

* **Span**: scope/horizon of concern  
* **Resolution**: granularity of distinctions and controllable variables  
* **Intensity**: reliability/strength of error-correction across span  
  :: not emotional intensity, but agency or control power

  #### Q6 Stress & frame collision

Stress often appears when overlapping frames imply incompatible actions/goals/constraints. The system pays an “inference tax” trying to reconcile them; chronic collision yields rigidity, oscillation, or burnout.

Stress is telemetry; harm happens when the signal stays high and the system can’t resolve it (no viable world/map/goal move).

**Interventions: world-change / map-change / goal-change**

##### Q7 Inscription (path dependence)

Interpretations and actions leave traces—habits, narratives, institutional rules, bodily adaptations—that bias future framing. The past becomes a constraint on the future.

##### Q8 Frame Lab (how to make it scientific)

Treat frames as hypotheses. Predict signatures: if you change the frame (goals, constraints, affordances, priors), you should see predictable shifts in attention, felt options, behavior, learning, and recovery under perturbations.

Falsifier example: if we predict that expanding affordances reduces stress, but stress remains unchanged after increasing real options and reducing constraints, then ‘stress \= inference-gap’ is incomplete in this regime.

#### Free Will as Frame Fluency

##### FW1 The Question Reframed

{eng} flags engineering claims
{core} flags core claims
{method} flags methodology

* **Inefficiency is the substrate of Freedom.**  
* **The competency of a system hides the system's inefficiency from evolution.**
* **Free Will isn't Free Start, it's Free Won't**

Whether we actually have it or not, I think the **feeling of free will** as qualia is a drive or motivator for optimization. Ask the average person what free will is, and one common answer is the ability to have chosen differently for some fork in the road. Unsaid but often implied is "given more information". If you're making choices without really thinking, letting the smaller intelligences within you run the whole show, how free can you really be?

The traditional free will debate asks: "Are our choices caused or uncaused?"
The frame perspective asks a different question: **"What capacities vary when we talk about freedom, and how do they work?"**

This isn't a dodge. It's recognizing that "free will" bundles together several distinct phenomena that the debate often conflates:

* The *phenomenology* of choosing (what deliberation feels like from the inside)  
* *Moral responsibility* (when praise/blame/accountability apply)  
* *Counterfactual capacity* (could you have done otherwise?)  
* *Self-authorship* (are your choices "yours" in some meaningful sense?)

Each of these is real. They just don't all require the same metaphysical commitments.

Free will, if it exists, **lives in the gap between choice and affordance**. More gap, more free will. The gap isn't necessarily temporal, but it's more the number of layers in play. Time just orders those layers in sequence. It is our post-hoc sense-maker, the narrator, that gives us most of our freedom. Not because it calls the shots in the moment, but because it terraforms the playground.

---

##### FW2 What's Actually Varying

{eng} When we argue about "free will," we're usually tracking real variance in these system properties:

**Frame awareness**: the capacity to notice you're operating within a frame rather than just seeing through it. This is rare. Most cognition is transparent—the frame is invisible, and the world just *seems* a certain way.

**Frame fluency**: the capacity to shift frames when the current one isn't serving you. This requires:

* Having alternative frames available  
* Meta-cognitive skill to recognize when a shift is needed  
* Low enough inscription that the old frame doesn't just snap back

**Light cone scope**: how much of the causal landscape you're representing. A wider light cone means more affordances are visible, more futures can be weighted, more constraints can be navigated. This is what "seeing options" actually is—not accessing some menu outside causation, but rendering more of the possibility space.

**Inscription depth**: how thoroughly your current frames have been written into substrate—neural, social, environmental. Deep inscription means high path-dependence; shallow inscription means more pivot capacity.

{core} These capacities are *real and variable*. Someone with high frame fluency and wide light cone navigating shallow inscription has genuine degrees of freedom that someone frame-locked doesn't. That difference matters—for ethics, for intervention design, for self-understanding.

---

##### FW3 Goals Don't Get Chosen From a Menu

{core} Here's where the framework diverges from folk intuition: goals *emerge* from constraint satisfaction within a frame. They're not selected by some prior self standing outside the frame, consulting preferences.

Change the constraints, change the projections, and different goals become attractive—not because you "decided" differently, but because the optimization landscape shifted. The phenomenology of "choosing" is real; it's the feeling of a frame settling into coherence. But it's not a ghost in the machine pulling levers from nowhere.

This sounds deflationary, but it's actually where freedom *lives*.

If goals were truly uncaused, you couldn't cultivate them, couldn't shape them through practice, couldn't be responsible for them in any meaningful sense. The fact that goals emerge from frame dynamics is what makes self-authorship *possible*.

---

##### FW4 Inscription: Where Path-Dependence Lives

{eng} Every action writes traces into substrates—neural patterns, social expectations, institutional rules, physical environments. Those traces make certain future frames more accessible, others less so.

This is why choices feel consequential: they *are*. Not because they originate from some uncaused source, but because they shape the frame-space you'll be navigating next. You're not just choosing an action; you're inscribing the conditions for future choosing.

**{core} Freedom, then, isn't freedom *from* inscription. It's the capacity to inscribe deliberately—to shape the constraints that will shape you.**

This reframes "self-control" from "overriding your desires" to "shaping the inscription landscape so different desires emerge."

"Deliberately" is doing a lot of work here. You're not writing on blank substrate. You're negotiating with inscriptions that are already optimizing. Deliberate inscription is diplomacy, not authorship.

Freedom is real but it's *contested*, not sovereign.

---

##### FW5 The Multi-Scale Problem

{core} Individual frame fluency is necessary but not sufficient. Frames aren't just in heads—they're inscribed in teams, institutions, economies, built environments. You can have perfect individual awareness and still be re-inscribed by structures that enforce the old frame.

This is where the Cassandra/Berserker dynamic bites:

* **Cassandras** have wide light cones but low inscription power—they see the problem but can't change the substrate.  
* **Berserkers** have narrow frames but high inscription power—they reshape the world according to a frame they can't see past.

**{method}** Collective free will—the capacity of groups to shift frames together—requires different interventions than individual frame fluency. It requires changing pivot spaces, governance structures, the substrates themselves.

"Personal responsibility" discourse often functions to obscure this. It's not wrong that individuals have agency; it's incomplete. The frame-aware question is always: at what scale is intervention most leveraged?

---

##### FW6 The Practical Reframe

**{method}** Instead of asking "was that choice free?" ask:

1. **What frame generated this choice?** (P, A, Π, G, C)  
2. **What made this frame feel obvious/inevitable?**  
3. **What inscription is this choice laying down?**  
4. **Is there a different frame where different actions become thinkable?**  
5. **What would it take to shift—individually and collectively?**

**{core} Cognitive freedom isn't freedom from causation. It's freedom to shift frames—which is itself a capacity that can be cultivated, atrophied, or structurally constrained.**

##### FW8 The Mechanics of Freedom

How to build it, not just argue about it.

1. Inefficiency is the Substrate. A perfectly efficient system has zero degrees of freedom (it must always take the optimal path). To have Agency, a system must have Slack—resources not currently fully utilized, allowing for exploration, error, and choice. Optimization kills freedom.

2. The Veto Power ("Free Won't"). You do not choose your thoughts; your Generators (System 1) do. Your Agency lives in the Veto—the capacity to inhibit an impulse before it becomes an inscription. You are not the author of the urge; you are the editor of the action.

3. Structural Depth (The Ordering of Layers). Freedom is proportional to the number of arbitration layers between Stimulus and Response.

    Reflex: Depth 0. (Stimulus → Action). The system is transparent to the input.

    Agency: Depth N. (Stimulus → Layer 1 → Layer 2... → Action). The input is de-correlated from the output by successive re-processings.

    "Time" is just the experience of traversing these layers.

###### 1. For the Compatibilist

*The Position:* Freedom is acting according to your own desires without coercion.

* **The Translation:** You are right. But where do "your own desires" come from? They emerge from your current Frame.
* **The Upgrade:** Your "Reasons-Responsive Agent" is simply a system with **High Frame Fluency**. It can inspect its own frame, notice errors, and update. A "coerced" agent is just one that is **Frame-Locked** by external constraints.

###### 2. For the Hard Determinist

*The Position:* Every state has a prior cause; therefore, freedom is an illusion.

* **The Translation:** You are right. The physics is closed. But there is a massive engineering difference between a calculator (1 frame) and a general-intelligence agent (many frames).
* **The Upgrade:** Determinism is true, but **Degrees of Freedom** are real system properties. A system that can model 50 futures has more "freedom" than a rock, even if both are determined. We call this **Lightcone Scope**.

###### 3. For the Libertarian

*The Position:* I feel like I am choosing; the future is open.

* **The Translation:** You are right about the *feeling*. That phenomenology is real.
* **The Upgrade:** That "openness" is the felt sense of **High Resolution + Low Inscription**. You are running multiple frames in parallel (simulation), and the "choice" is the moment the system settles into a stable attractor. It’s not magic; it’s **Arbitration**.

###### 4. For the Buddhist / No-Self Theorist

*The Position:* The "Self" is an illusion; there is no doer, only doing.

* **The Translation:** You are right. There is no homunculus.
* **The Upgrade:** The "Self" is a **User Interface (UI)** for the arbitration loop. It’s a useful fiction for coordination. "Enlightenment" is just **Maximum Frame Awareness**—seeing the UI as a UI, rather than mistaking it for the user.

###### 5. For the Existentialist

*The Position:* You are condemned to be free; you must create your own meaning.

* **The Translation:** You are right. You cannot *not* have a frame. Even "doing nothing" is a frame.
* **The Upgrade:** "Radical Freedom" is **Frame Fluency** in a low-inscription environment. "Authenticity" is taking responsibility for the **Inscriptions** you leave. You are the architect of your own constraints.

###### 6. For the Control Theorist

*The Position:* It's all just feedback loops and error minimization.

* **The Translation:** You are right. It is *all* control theory.
* **The Upgrade:** "Free Will" is just **Model-Based Control** with **Counterfactual Search**. We just skinned it as a D&D game to make the monkeys read it.

**Cognitive freedom isn't freedom from causation. It's the freedom to shift frames.**

* **Low Freedom:** Trapped in one view, one set of affordances, one set of "have-tos."
