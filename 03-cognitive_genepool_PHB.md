**Editor’s Note: On Session Zero**

Before the rules, before the tools, before the monsters, there is a Session Zero.

In tabletop games, Session Zero is where players agree on what kind of game they are about to play: what’s in scope, what isn’t, how power is used, and how to stop if something goes wrong. It’s not part of the adventure. It’s what makes the adventure survivable.

We use Session Zero in this project for the same reason.

The Cognitive Genepool is not a single theory or worldview. It’s a collection of lenses, patterns, and interventions meant to be picked up and put down. Some are powerful. Some are dangerous if misapplied. Some work well in one regime and fail badly in another.

Session Zero exists to:
- align expectations before tools are used,
- surface known failure modes early,
- make exit and refusal legitimate,
- and prevent the framework itself from becoming a monster.

Each book has its own Session Zero because each one grants a different kind of power:
- the Player’s Handbook offers ways of interpreting yourself,
- the Monster Manual offers names for recurring failures,
- the Dungeon Master’s Guide offers tools for intervening in systems that affect others.

The risks are different. The guardrails must be too.

Session Zero is not doctrine.
It is not required belief.
It is not a test of seriousness.

It is a consent check.

If you already know how to use these tools responsibly, you can skim or skip it. If you don’t, it may save you time, harm, or confusion later.

If at any point the tools in this project reduce your sense of agency, increase certainty without increasing leverage, or feel compulsory rather than optional, that is a signal to stop.

Putting the book down is always a valid move.

That, too, is part of the game.

## 0\. — Session Zero: How to Play *This* Game

> *Before you roll stats, agree on the kind of game you’re playing.*

This book is not here to tell you who you are.
It’s here to help you **operate a mind that is not a single character**, in environments that do not care about your intentions.

Before you use any tools in this book, pause and read this section.
It will save you time, confusion, and at least one unnecessary existential spiral.

---

## 0.1 What Kind of Game This Is

This is a **field manual**, not a belief system.

* You are not asked to accept any metaphysics.
* You are not asked to commit to a theory of self.
* You are not asked to explain your past.

You are asked to **try things** and notice what changes.

If a tool:

* does not change what you notice,
* does not change what you predict,
* does not change what you do,

…you should drop it.

That is not failure.
That is correct use.

---

## 0.2 The Player Is a Party (Not a Problem)

This book addresses the post-hoc cartographic function of System 2: the role that updates maps, chooses inscriptions, and reshapes future constraints. It does not assume a sovereign real-time controller.

This book treats “you” as a **coalition**:

* drives,
* habits,
* subroutines,
* stress responses,
* learned heuristics.

This is not to deny agency.
It is to make agency *operable*.

**Important:**
This framing is optional.

If thinking in terms of “parts,” “sub-agents,” or “inner characters”:

* increases clarity → keep it
* increases fragmentation or distress → stop immediately

Use a simpler frame. The goal is **coordination**, not introspection depth.

---

## 0.3 What This Book Is Not For

Do **not** use this book to:

* win arguments with yourself or others
* diagnose yourself or anyone else
* justify harm or avoidance
* narrate why you “can’t” change
* optimize yourself into exhaustion

If you notice yourself doing any of the above, close the book.

That’s not a moral judgment.
It’s a stop condition.

---

## 0.4 Safety & Exit Rules (Read This Carefully)

You should **pause or stop using this book** if:

* you feel less agency, not more
* you start narrating yourself as a monster
* every behavior starts looking pathological
* insight increases but action does not
* the framework feels compulsory

If that happens:

* return to concrete actions (sleep, food, movement, social contact)
* use a simpler model
* or put the book down entirely

No tool in this book is worth your well-being.

---

## 0.5 How to Actually Use the Player’s Handbook

Recommended mode:

1. Pick **one** tool
2. Use it for **one week**
3. Observe what changes
4. Keep it only if it earns its keep

You do not need:

* to read the book in order
* to understand everything
* to agree with the framing

You only need to notice whether it helps.

This book will:

* anthropomorphize patterns

That does **not** mean:

*What's rigid vs. flexible in your value stack*

**Test:** Imagine someone offering you $10M to give up each goal. If you wouldn't take the deal at ANY price, it's terminal. If you’d take the deal because it helps you achieve the same value another way, it might be instrumental.

#### Instrumental Goals (Means to Ends)

**Question:** What do you want BECAUSE it helps you get something else?

These are strategies, not values. They're replaceable if a better path appears.

1. ---

2. ---

3. ---

**Test:** If you found another way to achieve your terminal goals, would you still pursue this? If no, it's instrumental.

#### The Dangerous Confusion

**Common mistake:** Treating instrumental goals as terminal

Examples:

- "I must get promoted" (probably instrumental—you want security/status/impact)  
- "I must use this specific framework" (probably instrumental—you want understanding/results)  
- "I must fix this relationship" (might be terminal OR instrumental—check honestly)

**Why it matters:** You can negotiate instrumental goals. You can't negotiate terminal ones. Misclassifying them creates impossible conflicts.

**Diagnostic:**

- Are you suffering because you're defending an instrumental goal as if it were terminal?  
- Are you sacrificing terminal goals to protect instrumental strategies?

### SECTION 3: RE-SPEC DIFFICULTY

*What can and can't be changed about you*

Rate yourself on these three levels:

#### Goal-Level Change

**Question:** How *plastic* do your deepest priorities feel over years? Or, how persuadable are you at the values layer?

- ☐ **High:** I frequently update what I fundamentally care about  
- ☐ **Medium:** I've changed core values a few times in my life  
- ☐ **Low:** My deepest values have been stable for years/decades  
- ☐ **Locked:** There is nothing that could make me want different things at the root level

#### Method-Level Change

**Question:** Can you change HOW you pursue your goals?

- ☐ **High:** I'm constantly trying new strategies and approaches  
- ☐ **Medium:** I can switch tactics when something isn't working  
- ☐ **Low:** I tend to keep doing the same thing even when it fails  
- ☐ **Locked:** I know only one way to approach problems in this domain

#### Model-Level Change

**Question:** Can you update your beliefs about how the world works?

- ☐ **High:** I actively seek evidence that contradicts my beliefs  
- ☐ **Medium:** I update when presented with strong evidence  
- ☐ **Low:** I'm defensive when my worldview is challenged  
- ☐ **Locked:** I refuse to consider alternatives

#### What This Reveals

**If you're Low/Locked on goals but High on methods:**

- You have strong values but can be flexible in how you pursue them  
- **Good news:** Intervention can work through strategy changes  
- **Risk:** Make sure your methods actually serve your goals (check for Goodhart/metric fixation)

**If you're Low/Locked on methods but High on models:**

- You learn new facts easily but struggle to change approaches  
- **Pattern risk:** The Ossified Scaffold (tool becomes prison)  
- **Intervention needed:** Practice strategy switching, seek alternative methods

**If you're Low/Locked across all three:**

- You're in a rigid attractor state  
- **Pattern risk:** Multiple (Cirrhotic Liver if substrate-locked, Maximizer if goal-locked)  
- **Intervention needed:** World-change or substrate-change (changing environment or yourself physically)

**If you're High across all three:**

- Maximum adaptability OR lack of stable identity  
- **Risk:** No inscription depth \= can't build on past learning  
- **Intervention needed:** Deliberately cultivate some stable commitments

#### The Counterfactual Thickness Test

**If you listed 1-2 options:** Low agency

- You're in a narrow corridor  
- **Pattern risk:** Feeling trapped, powerless  
- **Intervention:** World-change (create new affordances) or Map-change (generate more options)

**If you listed 3-5 options:** Moderate agency

- Normal range for most problems  
- Check: Are these ACTUALLY different, or variations on the same approach?

**If you listed 6+ options:** High agency OR you're overthinking

- Either: You have real optionality  
- Or: You're generating options to avoid committing (analysis paralysis)  
- **Test:** Can you actually EXECUTE any of these, or are they all hypothetical?

#### The Constraint Audit

**Question:** What's actually preventing you from solving this problem?

Check all that apply:

- ☐ **Resources** (time, money, energy, materials)  
- ☐ **Skills** (don't know how to do what's needed)  
- ☐ **Information** (don't know what to do)  
- ☐ **Authority** (not allowed to act)  
- ☐ **Substrate** (physical/institutional architecture blocks it)  
- ☐ **Coordination** (need others to cooperate)  
- ☐ **Motivation** (know what to do but can't make yourself do it)

**Each constraint type requires a different intervention:**

- Resources → World-change (get resources or change goal)  
- Skills → Map-change (learn) or World-change (get help)  
- Information → Map-change (research, ask)  
- Authority → World-change (negotiate, circumvent) or Goal-change (pursue what you CAN do)  
- Substrate → Usually requires substrate replacement (rare but critical)  
- Coordination → World-change (align incentives) or Map-change (find solo path)  
- Motivation → This is complex—see Who is Taking Point section

### SECTION 5: MONSTERS IN THE WALLS

*Which monsters are running inside you*

Rate how often you experience each pattern (0 \= never, 5 \= constantly):

#### The Metric Maximizer

*Optimizing a proxy at the expense of the actual goal*

\_\_/5  I focus on measurable metrics even when I know they don't fully capture what matters \_\_/5  I game my own systems (e.g., checking off tasks that don't matter to feel productive)  
\_\_/5  I've caught myself optimizing "likes" or "followers" or "inbox zero" as if they were the point

**If 3+:** You're at risk. Check whether your instrumental goals have replaced your terminal ones.

#### The Cassandra

*Seeing problems you can't fix, converting awareness into suffering*

\_\_/5  I'm aware of problems (climate, politics, organizational dysfunction) I can't personally solve \_\_/5  I feel guilty or anxious about things outside my control \_\_/5  People tell me I'm "too negative" or "always worrying"

**If 3+:** You're in the Plane of Powerlessness. Shrink your lightcone to what you can actually affect, or find a lever.

#### The Ossified Scaffold

*Trapped by tools that used to help*

\_\_/5  I have methods/frameworks I can't think without, even when they're not helping \_\_/5  I defend my approach even when results are poor \_\_/5  I struggle to articulate alternatives to my current way of doing things

**If 3+:** Your scaffolds are capturing you. Practice deliberately using different tools/frameworks.

#### The Captured Narrator

*System 2 recruited to justify rather than evaluate*

\_\_/5  I produce elaborate reasons for decisions I've already made  
\_\_/5  I have trouble generating arguments against my own position \_\_/5  I'm better at explaining why I'm right than considering if I might be wrong

**If 3+:** Your deliberation has become justification. Practice counterfactual generation before committing.

#### The Cirrhotic Substrate

*Inscribed damage that blocks healing*

\_\_/5  Past trauma/injury affects my current function even when I want to move on \_\_/5  I know what healthy behavior looks like but physically/neurologically can't execute it consistently \_\_/5  Healing attempts seem to create new problems

**If 3+:** You may have substrate-level damage. This requires more than willpower—consider whether you need intervention from professionals (remember that all intelligence is collective).

### SECTION 6: WHO IS TAKING POINT

*Control Ecology. Who's driving right now?*

#### Your Internal Collective

**Question:** Which subsystems are most active in your decision-making?

Rate each 0-5 for how much influence they have:

**Generators (Fast, automatic responses):** \_\_/5  Habits and reflexes \_\_/5  Emotional reactions  
\_\_/5  Dopamine-seeking \_\_/5  Threat avoidance \_\_/5  Social conformity

**Navigator (Real-time arbitration):** \_\_/5  Conscious choice between competing impulses \_\_/5  "Should I do X or Y right now?" decisions \_\_/5  Crisis response and triage

**Cartographer (Post-hoc sense-making):** \_\_/5  Explaining my behavior to myself \_\_/5  Updating my self-narrative \_\_/5  Learning from experience

#### The Balance Question

**Healthy:** Navigator and Cartographer active, Generators providing options

- You feel agency, you update beliefs, you learn

**Generator-Dominated:** Acting on autopilot, minimal conscious choice

- **Risk:** Habit loops, addiction patterns, zombie mode  
- **Intervention:** Increase Navigator activation (meditation, pauses, explicit choice points)

**Navigator-Dominated:** Constant effortful decision-making, everything requires willpower

- **Risk:** Decision fatigue, burnout, paralysis  
- **Intervention:** Build better habits (offload to Generators), simplify environment

**Cartographer-Dominated:** Endless analysis, story-making about why things happened

- **Risk:** Rumination, analysis paralysis, post-hoc justification  
- **Intervention:** Action before perfect understanding, empiricism over narrative
