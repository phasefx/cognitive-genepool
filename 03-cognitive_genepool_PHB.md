# The Player’s Handbook  
## A Manual of Agency — Operating Yourself as a Collective

---

## This Is Not a Game

The D&D language in this book exists for two reasons:

1. **Leverage:** Metaphors compress patterns into handles.  
2. **Approachability:** It makes self-examination feel less clinical and more usable.

What it is *not* for:

- Leveling up
- Optimizing your “build”
- Collecting achievements
- Identifying as a type

You are not a Wizard.  
You are not a Paladin.

You are a system that sometimes processes *like* a Wizard, sometimes *like* a Paladin.  
The classes are **verbs, not nouns** — modes you can notice, invite, and swap.

If you find yourself min-maxing this book, the Artificer has captured you.

## 0. Session Zero — The Player Contract

Before the tools, before the monsters, before the metaphors, there is a Session Zero.

This book offers ways of interpreting yourself. That is a real power, and it comes with risks.

### 0.1 What This Is (and Is Not)

This book is a **field manual**, not a belief system.

- You are not asked to accept a metaphysics  
- You are not asked to explain your past  
- You are not asked to diagnose yourself  

You are asked to **try things** and notice what changes.

If a tool in this book:

- does not change what you notice  
- does not change what you predict  
- does not change what you do  

…drop it.

That is not failure.  
That is correct use.

---

### 0.2 The Player Is a Party

This book treats “you” as a **coalition**:

- drives  
- habits  
- stress responses  
- learned heuristics  

This framing is **optional**.

If thinking in terms of parts or modes:

- increases clarity → keep it  
- increases fragmentation or distress → stop immediately  

Use a simpler model.  
The goal is **coordination**, not introspection depth.

---

### 0.3 What This Book Is Not For

Do not use this book to:

- win arguments with yourself or others  
- narrate why you “can’t” change  
- optimize yourself into exhaustion  
- assign identities to people  

If you notice yourself doing any of the above, close the book.

---

### 0.4 Safety & Exit Rules

Pause or stop using this book if:

- you feel less agency, not more  
- every behavior starts looking pathological  
- insight increases but action does not  
- the framework feels compulsory  

If that happens:

- return to concrete actions (sleep, food, movement)  
- switch to a simpler model  
- or put the book down entirely  

Putting the book down is always a valid move.

## 1. The Player Is Not One Thing

You are not a unitary controller.

You are a system that runs **different cognitive modes** depending on context and stress.

These modes are not personalities or traits.  
They are **ways of processing reality** that are useful in some regimes and harmful in others.

Pathology often emerges when:
- one mode dominates under stress, and
- refuses to relinquish control.

This book exists to help you **notice which mode is driving**, and whether it’s still appropriate.

## 2. The Minimal Model (What You Need to Use the Classes)

This is the *smallest amount of theory required* to make the rest of the book operable.

### 2.1 Frames

A **frame** is the bundle that makes “the world” show up in a usable way.

Compact form:

**F = (P, A, Π, G, C)**

- **P — Projection:** what gets rendered / noticed  
- **A — Affordances:** what actions feel possible  
- **Π — Priors / Policy:** default expectations and habits  
- **G — Goals:** what counts as success  
- **C — Constraints:** what cannot be violated  

These are **helicopter controls**.  
They are coupled — changing one shifts the others.

---

### 2.2 Encode / Decode

- **Encode:** learning and compression (habits, memory, parameters)  
- **Decode:** rendering that latent state into a felt action-space  

Your subjective experience is the *felt shape* of the decoded affordance landscape.

---

### 2.3 Concern and Competence

Two distinct properties:

- **Concern:** how large a goal-space the system can meaningfully care about  
- **Competence:** how effectively it can navigate that space under stress  

Mismatches matter:

- High concern + low competence → paralysis (Cassandra)  
- High competence + narrow concern → damage (Berserker)

> [BOUNDARY NOTE]  
> This book uses these terms descriptively.  
> Formal definitions and rigor live in the SRD.

---

### 2.4 Inscription

Actions leave traces.

Habits, tools, narratives, environments, and scars bias future frames.

**Inscription doesn’t argue for itself.  
It makes alternatives harder to think.**

## 3. The Six Cognitive Classes  
### Modes You Can Notice, Invite, and Swap

Each class is a **stabilized cognitive mode**.

Everyone has access to all of them.  
The question is which one is currently driving — and what it’s costing you.

---

### The Wizard — *Projection*

**Mode:** Map-making, modeling, abstraction  

**Strength:**  
Sees structure others miss. Builds coherent models.

**Signature Cost:**  
May get lost in the map and forget the territory.

**Under Stress Becomes:**  
**The Reboot Loop** — infinite re-analysis.

**When to Invite the Wizard:**
- When reality feels confusing or incoherent  
- When you need to understand why something works

---

### The Ranger — *Affordance*

**Mode:** Map-contact, intuition, external signal  

**Strength:**  
Reads the room. Navigates novel terrain quickly.

**Signature Cost:**  
Often can’t justify or explain the gut read.

**Under Stress Becomes:**  
**The Cassandra** — sees the signal, has no lever.

**When to Invite the Ranger:**
- When you’re overthinking  
- When you need fresh signal from outside your head

---

### The Bard — *Frame*

**Mode:** Lens-shifting, narration, reframing  

**Strength:**  
Changes what the situation *means*.

**Signature Cost:**  
Can mistake eloquence for truth.

**Under Stress Becomes:**  
**The Captured Narrator** — defending a story instead of looking.

**When to Invite the Bard:**
- When everything feels stuck or overdetermined  
- When a new story could open options

---

### The Artificer — *Inscription*

**Mode:** Scaffold-building, tools, systems  

**Strength:**  
Turns effort into autopilot. Extends capacity.

**Signature Cost:**  
Tools can ossify and become prisons.

**Under Stress Becomes:**  
**The Ossified Scaffold** — the tool becomes the world.

**When to Invite the Artificer:**
- When willpower isn’t enough  
- When you need reliability, not insight

---

### The Paladin — *Goals & Constraints*

**Mode:** Line-holding, commitment, shipping  

**Strength:**  
Finishes things. Resists drift.

**Signature Cost:**  
Can rigidify and moralize.

**Under Stress Becomes:**  
**The Berserker** — narrow optimization, collateral damage.

**When to Invite the Paladin:**
- When things keep slipping  
- When a finish line is needed

---

### The Rogue — *Priors*

**Mode:** Edge-finding, counterfactuals  

**Strength:**  
Breaks habits. Questions the obvious.

**Signature Cost:**  
Can over-index on hidden explanations.

**Under Stress Becomes:**  
**The Paranoid Generator** — secret patterns justify fear.

**When to Invite the Rogue:**
- When nothing seems to work  
- When you suspect the game is being gamed

## 4. From Class to Monster  
### Stress Responses, Not Evil

A **monster** is what happens when a class:

- is under sustained stress, and  
- refuses to relinquish control.

Monsters are **verbs, not identities**.

You do not defeat your own monster with the same mode that summoned it.  
You must **multi-class** — invite a different mode to the table.

> [BOUNDARY NOTE]  
> This book names monsters only to support self-navigation.  
> Full diagnostics live in the Monster Manual.

These are **moves**, not traits.

> [BOUNDARY NOTE]

## 6. Scaffolds  
### Equipment That Extends Without Capturing

Scaffolds are tools that offload cognition:

- habits  
- checklists  
- social contracts  
- environments  

They are powerful — and dangerous.

Over time, scaffolds can ossify, narrowing what you can see or do.

Wisdom is **inscription efficiency**:
what you inscribe well enough that you don’t have to re-derive it.
